## Claim

Constitutional Capitalism’s Local Food and Family Farm Prosperity System (CC-PRIN-44 / CC-DEC-089) claims that rebuilding locally rooted family farms — through fair market access, regional processing and distribution, stewardship rewards, ownership pathways, and institutions that keep prosperity circulating locally — can make family farms engines of community wealth, public health, land stewardship, and resilience.

This packet investigates the **research question** below. It does **not** treat architecture documents as evidence.

## Research Question

Can locally rooted family farm systems supported by regional processing, institutional purchasing, regenerative stewardship, and cooperative infrastructure create broader and more durable community prosperity than comparable conventional systems under identifiable conditions?

## Failure Conditions

Doctrine (as a prosperity system) is unlikely to work where:

- No regional processing or aggregation
- No transportation / cold chain
- No institutional buyers or unreliable procurement budgets
- Poor cooperative / utility governance
- Weak local demand
- Insufficient workforce or entrepreneurship
- Capital shortages for mid-tier infrastructure
- “Local” rules gamed without farmer income gains
- Cost premiums crowd out meal program participation without offsetting funds

These are **logical/design failure conditions** derived from architecture notes and claim uncertainties — not yet a registered empirical failure corpus.

## Final Verdict

**Qualifies**

### Supports

Not selected.

### Qualifies

**Selected.** Registered federal statistics and program descriptions **support narrow predicates** of the architecture:

1. Family farms remain central to U.S. farm structure (with production concentrated among larger operations).
2. Institutional local purchasing is already a material, widespread practice in school food.
3. Federal tools already target local/regional market and mid-tier infrastructure gaps.

They **do not** establish that the full HYP-116 package — guaranteed institutional markets + shared Food Utility infrastructure + stewardship pay — creates **broader and more durable community prosperity** than comparable conventional systems under identifiable conditions.

Therefore the architectural hypothesis is **qualified**: treat CC-PRIN-44 as an architecture and research agenda with descriptive baselines, not as an empirically demonstrated prosperity system. Purchase floors and Food Utility Cooperatives remain design parameters requiring modeling, legal feasibility work, contrary literature registration, and field research (including LCLs as investigations, not pilots).

### Contradicts

Not selected. Registered contrary notes qualify and bound the claim; they do not falsify the entire hypothesis given the thin corpus.
