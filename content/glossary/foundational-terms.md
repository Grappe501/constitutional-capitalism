# Foundational Terms

> These are Phase 1 working definitions, not final sourced legal or scholarly definitions.

## Constitutional Capitalism
A developing framework that joins secure private property and free enterprise with constitutional limits on public and private domination.

## Capitalism
An economic order characterized by private ownership of productive assets, markets, investment, and wage labor; its institutional forms vary.

## Private property
Legally protected control and use of assets, subject to lawful duties, due process, and rights of others.

## Free enterprise
The liberty to form, operate, innovate, and exchange through lawful businesses.

## Broad ownership
Widely distributed, meaningful access to homes, savings, businesses, and productive assets.

## Economic power
The capacity to shape others’ options, markets, communities, or institutions through control of economic resources.

## Political domination
Control that defeats equal citizenship or lawful democratic accountability.

## Economic domination
Durable economic control that leaves people, firms, or communities without realistic alternatives.

## Responsible ownership
Ownership exercised with lawful accountability for foreseeable harms and public privileges received.

## Fair competition
Rivalry under rules that protect open entry, honest dealing, and contestable markets.

## Anti-concentration
The constitutional aim of preventing durable private power from dominating markets or politics.

## Market-access responsibility
Reciprocal duties arising from use of legal privileges and public conditions that enable market participation.

## Limited and capable government
Government constrained by law yet competent to enforce general rules and protect rights.

## Socialism
A family of systems that place productive assets substantially under social or state ownership or control.

## Social democracy
A market economy paired with extensive social protections, collective bargaining, and redistributive public policy.

## Democratic socialism
A democratic program seeking substantial social ownership or control of productive assets.

## Ordoliberalism
A tradition favoring a strong legal framework to preserve competitive markets and prevent private and public power abuses.

## Distributism
A tradition favoring widespread ownership of productive property, often through small enterprises and associations.

## Employee ownership
A range of arrangements through which employees hold equity or beneficial interests in their employer.

## Profit sharing
A compensation arrangement that distributes a defined share of business profits to workers or other participants.

## Constitutional republic
A representative political order governed by a constitution that limits and allocates public authority.

## Democratic accountability
The ability of citizens to understand, contest, oversee, and lawfully change governing rules and officials.

## Local economic resilience
A community’s capacity to sustain livelihoods and adapt to economic disruptions.

## Intergenerational stewardship
A duty to avoid imposing avoidable costs or foreclosing opportunity for future people.

## Destination-based taxation
A tax approach that assigns tax liability by the location of consumption or destination rather than production or origin.
