## First Principle

> The purpose of a free society is to maximize the ability of every person and every community to build, share, and sustain prosperity through liberty, responsibility, opportunity, and voluntary cooperation.

If a doctrine item cannot be derived from this First Principle (and the axioms below), it should be questioned.

Related existing commitments (not replaced):

- Purpose of an economy: a prosperous, free, and self-governing people
- People as the nation’s greatest source of productive capacity — with dignity guardrail
- Sacred sentence: power should always flow toward the people

## The Ten Axioms

### 1. Definition of Prosperity

Prosperity is broader than income: financial security, physical and mental health, strong families, educational opportunity, community trust, property and enterprise ownership, civic participation, environmental stewardship, freedom and dignity, and generational mobility.

Prosperity is **not** consumption alone, GDP alone, government spending alone, or wealth concentration alone.

### 2. Role of Government

Government exists to establish the rules, infrastructure, and institutions that allow free people and free communities to flourish while protecting liberty, justice, and fair competition.

Not “big government” or “small government” as ends.

### 3. Role of Markets

Competitive markets are the preferred mechanism for allocating most goods and services, provided they operate under transparent rules, protect competition, and do not permit the concentration of power that undermines liberty or equal opportunity.

Markets are tools — neither inherently virtuous nor inherently harmful.

### 4. Theory of Ownership

Ownership — personal, family, employee, cooperative, community, and public where appropriate — creates responsibility, accountability, and long-term investment in the success of people and places.

### 5. Intergenerational Responsibility

Every generation should leave its communities, institutions, natural resources, and public finances stronger than it inherited them.

### 6. Philosophy of Community

Communities are the primary institutions through which people develop trust, relationships, mutual aid, identity, and shared prosperity.

### 7. Liberty and Shared Responsibility

Liberty is essential. Responsibility gives liberty meaning. Communities thrive when people voluntarily contribute to institutions that benefit everyone — encouraged, not compulsory.

### 8. Philosophy of Human Capital

People are the nation’s greatest productive asset. Investment in people is not consumption; it is productive capital formation.

Dignity guardrail: a person is never merely a resource.

### 9. Ethical Guardrails

Never compromise: equal dignity; rule of law; due process; freedom of speech and conscience; private property; democratic accountability; transparency; anti-corruption; competition over favoritism.

### 10. Traceability and Operational Coherence

Applications must remain derivable from the First Principle and Axioms 1–9. Philosophy becomes stronger by becoming simpler and more coherent — not broader.
