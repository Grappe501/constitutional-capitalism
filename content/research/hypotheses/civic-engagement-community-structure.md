## Why this track exists

Constitutional Capitalism has built dense structure evidence — ownership, wealth, agriculture, healthcare, energy, journalism, legislative/campaign-finance infrastructure. The connective question is still open:

> What happens to civic participation when these conditions change?

Historic voting and participation data are the bridge — not as a slogan about turnout, but as a way to ask what environments produce engagement.

## Central question

> **What conditions consistently correlate with civic engagement, participation, trust, and community durability?**

Not only: “Did turnout go up?”

## Unifying culture question (shared with HYP-125/126/127)

> How do we create a culture where citizens believe self-government is something they do, not something they watch?

## Five layers (research design)

1. **Voting participation** — Arkansas county presidential/midterm/primary/local/school-board/initiative turnout, 2000–2024 where obtainable; derive trend, volatility, persistence.  
2. **Community structure overlays** — economic, ownership, healthcare, information, education, demography (reuse NASS/FDIC/HRSA/ACS/journalism joins; no proxy abuse).  
3. **Community Health Index** — **exploratory model only**, never a National Baseline scoreboard slot.  
4. **HYP-126 test surface** — which environments produce participation?  
5. **HYP-127 control surface** — adult civic baselines before attributing youth-suffrage outcomes to voting age alone.

## Epistemic walls

- Descriptive co-variation **before** causation  
- Turnout ≠ trust ≠ volunteerism ≠ resilience  
- Stable high turnout ≠ volatile medium turnout  
- Ownership/concentration overlays ≠ capture (CC-CLAIM-003)  
- Illustrative county sketches (Benton/Searcy/White) are **not measured results**
