## Central question

> How do we move power, ownership, capability, and decision-making closer to ordinary people without breaking constitutional protections, markets, or civil liberties?

## Role now

Bridge from diagnosis into implementation — a system that can eventually be **handed to an Arkansas administration**, not merely advocated.

## Why four layers

Many Arkansas transition pieces can be built under **existing law**; others need legislation, constitutional change, or long-horizon institutional work. Sequencing prevents all-at-once transformation risk.

| Layer | Horizon | Posture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Immediate programs | 0–4 years | Largely buildable under existing law |
| 2 Community wealth | 4–8 years | Entity/contract design; ownership of productive assets |
| 3 Civic infrastructure | 4–12 years | Participation **between** elections (HYP-126) |
| 4 Structural reform | 8–16 years | Legislation / constitutional / institutional — research-bound first |

## Implementation ladder

`local_pilot → measurable_result → replication → county_network → regional_system → statewide_system`

Climb only after measurable results and pre-registered expansion criteria.

## Intervention packet (required fields)

Need → local institution → pilot geography → existing authority → funding stack → partners → workforce pipeline → ownership model → measurable outcomes → legal barriers → pilot → expansion trigger → failure conditions.

Every packet also requires **`WHY_THIS_CAN_START_NOW`**:

- Existing authority  
- Administrative action  
- Legislative change required  
- Constitutional amendment potentially required  
- Federal waiver/program dependency  
- Cannot currently be implemented  

First packets: `data/project/arkansas_magnet_hub_intervention_packets.json`. Invent nothing.

## Layer 1 flagship — Rural Agriculture Magnet Hub

Not merely a school farm. A **community economic development engine** centered on the school: magnet agriculture, greenhouses, school farm, FFA/4-H, apprenticeships, food processing, business/marketing, AI/tech, skilled trades.

Converges with Rose Bud LCL (HYP-122), Rural Prosperity Campus (HYP-123), Distributed Arkansas Prosperity Network (HYP-124), and HYP-130 capacity gaps.

**Funding scan** (eligibility not yet proven): USDA (EQIP, CIG, RD, VAPG, LFPP, RFSP, Community Facilities, RBDG), Perkins V / magnet / workforce funds, Registered Apprenticeship & WIOA, Arkansas AEDC/Ag/CTE, and major rural philanthropies.

## Layers 2–4 (compressed)

- **L2:** Cooperative food network · local grocery cooperative · local capital pools — communities as owners, not only aid consumers.  
- **L3:** Community assemblies · participatory budgeting · nonpartisan civic networks — influence between elections; rights never depend on participation.  
- **L4:** Campaign finance (HYP-125) · labor pathways · education debt/pathways · local care networks and health workforce expansion — only after evidence and legal gates.

## Evidence-first expansion rule

Strongest current posture: pilot or program in **one** school district, town, or county; measure against **comparable communities** (HYP-128); expand only after results.

## What this is not

- Not a new principle or Declaration amendment  
- Not a secured grant portfolio  
- Not an all-at-once statewide transformation  
- Not a substitute for baseline definition-locks
