# CC-HYP-LOCAL-DEMOCRATIC-CAMPAIGN-FINANCE-SYSTEM (HYP-125)

**Status:** Major research hypothesis — **not doctrine** · **not a principle** · **not locked reform**  
**Update:** UPD-110 · Decision: CC-DEC-105 (`KEEP_AS_HYPOTHESIS`)

## Central hypothesis (testable)

> Can campaign-finance rules make political power substantially more local, transparent, citizen-driven, and competitive without allowing government to determine which political voices may be heard?

## Constitutional Capitalism test

> Can we design campaign finance so that money remains capable of supporting political speech, but wealth can no longer purchase anonymity, privileged access, or disproportionate control over the political information environment?

Verdicts: **Supports** · **Qualifies** · **Contradicts**.

## Hard distinctions (do not collapse)

1. **Eliminating dark money ≠ eliminating political spending.**  
   Target: every dollar used materially to influence an election should be traceable to its true human or organizational funding source — advertisement → spender → intermediary → funding organization → significant original sources. That is a disclosure problem distinct from banning speech.

2. **Locality ≠ banning out-of-state speech.**  
   Not: “Nobody outside Arkansas may talk about an Arkansas election.”  
   Target: an Arkansas voter giving $25 should have meaningful political agency even when national organizations are spending millions.

3. **Disclosure ≠ capture.**  
   OpenFEC can deepen disclosure of concentration and independent expenditures. `CC-CLAIM-003` remains **Not Enough Evidence** until causal capture is established.

## What this is not

- Not doctrine or a new principle  
- Not an endorsement of any constitutional amendment (including study objects such as S.J.Res.78)  
- Not a claim that outside money’s effects are already measured  
- Not a slogan lock for “make elections local again” until evidence graduates

## Design tracks

### Track A — under existing First Amendment doctrine

Disclosure, real-time reporting, beneficial-source transparency, enforcement, coordination definitions, small-donor matching, democracy credits/vouchers, local public financing, free/low-cost public communication infrastructure, debate/forum access, strong independent local journalism, inexpensive civic-information systems.

### Track B — constitutional architecture research if doctrine blocks the necessary design

Comparative amendments and state experiments that would authorize viewpoint-neutral election-finance rules and public financing while preserving freedom of the press. Study without adoption. Research cannot stop at *Citizens United*; the FEC notes that on **June 30, 2026**, the Supreme Court held federal limits on coordinated political-party expenditures unconstitutional.

## Journalism convergence

Campaign-finance reform must be studied with the local information economy (`CC-HYP-LOCAL-INDEPENDENT-JOURNALISM-ECOSYSTEM` and siblings). Reducing spending without local journalism, forums, voter guides, and open election data can perversely help incumbents and wealthy self-funders. Government must not choose winners in the press; the civic system can make it cheaper for citizens to learn who is running, who funded them, what they believe, what the office controls, and what incumbents have done.

## Empirical questions (must answer publicly)

- Local donor share vs out-of-state donor share?  
- Small-dollar share and donor concentration?  
- Independent expenditure / outside-spending share and concentration?  
- Candidate spending; contested vs uncontested races; competitiveness?  
- Incumbent vs challenger differences?  
- Heterogeneity across federal, state, and local races?  
- Does small-donor matching raise participation and/or polarization?  
- Do strict limits protect incumbents by raising discovery costs?  
- Does disclosure chill low-level contributions?  
- How does journalism coverage covary with these structures?
