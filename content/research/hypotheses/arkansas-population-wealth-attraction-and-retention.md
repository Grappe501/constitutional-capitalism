# CC-HYP-ARKANSAS-POPULATION-WEALTH-ATTRACTION-AND-RETENTION-SYSTEM (HYP-132)

**Status:** Research hypothesis — **not doctrine** · **not a principle** · **not an anti-mobility wall**  
**Update:** UPD-149 · Decision: CC-DEC-136 (`KEEP_AS_HYPOTHESIS`)

## Central questions

> What combination of infrastructure, services, opportunity, ownership, taxation, civic life, and quality of place causes households and productive capital to remain in, return to, and move into Arkansas—especially its smaller communities?

> Which interventions actually produce **permanent households** rather than temporary relocations?

## Strategic posture

> **No loopholes for wealth already subject to Arkansas law—but make Arkansas so desirable, productive, connected, and family-friendly that leaving is economically and personally unattractive.**

Primary retention strategy: **attraction, not coercion**.  
No loopholes ≠ no mobility. Migration evidence on taxes is **mixed** — do not lock a single elasticity.

## Three population goals (measure separately)

| Goal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Keep** | Stop losing young families and skilled people |
| **Return** | Give former Arkansans a reason to come home |
| **Attract** | Recruit families, entrepreneurs, workers, researchers, remote workers, capital |

Composition matters: retiree inflows ≠ 30-year-old families with children.

## Distributed Prosperity Network (links HYP-124)

Reject metro-only opportunity. Every region develops an authentic prosperity engine; connect them. Major state investment should ask whether it can strengthen a smaller hub **before** defaulting to existing metro concentration — without suppressing Little Rock or Northwest Arkansas.

Access standard: excellent education, healthcare, broadband, employment, culture, recreation, food, transportation, and civic life without needing a major urban center.

## Family-attraction package

Housing · broadband · schools · healthcare · childcare · safety/EMS · parks · local food/retail · remote-work infra · regional transport · journalism/civic networks · **opportunity to own something** (home, business, land, co-op, community equity, retirement wealth).

Remote work can seed growth; it cannot rebuild a community alone.

## Come Home to Arkansas (links HYP-130)

Shortage pipelines (doctor, dentist, nurse, engineer, teacher, trades, AI, farmer, vet, entrepreneur) paired with house + school + healthcare + profession + ownership + community — not merely a cash stipend.

## Social contract with wealth (links HYP-131)

Not punishment. Arkansas wants wealth built, invested, and lived here. Extraordinary surplus wealth contributes a small share to sustain the society that makes prosperity possible. Ordinary labor and ordinary homeownership remain protected by the residual-wealth floor.

### `ARKANSAS_ROOTEDNESS` (research variable — not locked)

Study whether verified Arkansas investments (workforce, productive enterprise, rural healthcare, housing, apprenticeships, infrastructure) could credit against some future residual wealth contribution — only with measurable public value, caps, and independent verification. Otherwise it becomes a loophole.

## Virtuous circle (links Public Income Stack)

Better communities → families & capital → labor & firms → External Income → public revenue → Prosperity Fund → lower compulsory burden → more attractiveness.

## State goal (hypothesis statement)

> Arkansas should become the easiest place in America to build a family, build a business, build wealth, belong to a community, and participate meaningfully in civic life—whether you live in a city of 200,000 or a town of 2,000.

If that is built, **wealth retention becomes a consequence of prosperity rather than a problem solved with walls.**
