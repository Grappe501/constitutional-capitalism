# CC-HYP-AGE-16-LOCAL-SUFFRAGE (HYP-127)

**Status:** Major empirical research hypothesis — **not doctrine** · **not a principle** · **not locked voting-age policy**  
**Update:** UPD-116 · Decision: CC-DEC-107 (`KEEP_AS_HYPOTHESIS`)  
**Constraint:** `CC-RDC-NO-KNOWLEDGE-QUALIFICATION-FOR-SUFFRAGE`  
**Module:** `CC-MOD-AGE-16-LOCAL-SUFFRAGE`  
**Demands:** `data/project/CC_AGE_16_LOCAL_SUFFRAGE_DATA_DEMANDS.json`

## Hard constraint (non-negotiable)

> Educate everyone; enfranchise by law — **not by examination**.

Civic education, testing, certification, GPA, service hours, or political knowledge shall **not** be prerequisites for the franchise.

## Central question

> **Under what institutional conditions does enfranchisement at 16 produce meaningful, durable civic participation?**

Not only: “Should people vote at 16?”

## Hypothesis (testable)

> Under identifiable institutional conditions — especially local decision-making, civic education, and dense civic networks — extending suffrage to 16- and 17-year-olds for local elections may strengthen meaningful and durable civic participation without converting civic preparation into a franchise gate.

Scope: **local elections first**. Not automatic federal or statewide expansion.

## Four propositions (separate verdicts allowed)

| ID | Proposition | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | A16-P-CAPABILITY | Do 16–17-year-olds make meaningfully lower-quality political choices? |
| Participation | A16-P-PARTICIPATION | Do they register and vote (vs overall and 18–24)? |
| Habit formation | A16-P-HABIT | Does 16–17 voting raise later adult habitual participation? |
| System effects | A16-P-SYSTEM | Does enfranchisement change schools, campaigns, journalism, families, candidates, or local government responsiveness? |

Acceptable outcome example: Capability **Supports** · Participation **Qualifies** · Habit **Not Enough Evidence** · System **Not Enough Evidence**.

## Sequencing assumption — now a test, not a lock

Earlier design preference:

> Build excellent universal civic education first → then extend local suffrage to 16.

That preference is now an **explicit research question**:

> Should civic education precede youth enfranchisement, or does enfranchisement itself make civic education more effective?

A 16-year-old learning about city council knowing “I’m voting in November” may engage differently from someone taught “you’ll use this someday.” Reality answers.

## Maryland as primary U.S. laboratory

Maryland does **not** generally enfranchise 16-year-olds for state/federal elections. Municipalities changed eligibility for their own elections.

Useful structure:

**municipality before reform → after reform → neighboring municipality without reform → later-adopting municipality**

Cases: Takoma Park (pioneer), Greenbelt (later adopter; 2019 first 16+ election), other MD municipalities, matched controls.

## Newark — contrary evidence kept visible

Newark school-board 2025: **1,851** youth registered; **73** voted (~**3.94%** of registered youth). Overall turnout ~**3.47%**.

Lesson under study (not slogan):

> Extending suffrage does not by itself repair a civic culture in which almost nobody participates.

Connects directly to `CC-HYP-DISTRIBUTED-CIVIC-COMMONS` (HYP-126): rights + education + networks + meaningful local institutions.

**Finding:** institutional coding is possible; **machine-ready age-stratified election series are almost entirely unpublished**. Takoma Park / Greenbelt are the best manual-extractable starts. Inventory ≠ measurement dataset. A positive youth-turnout anecdote must not promote habit formation or system-effects verdicts. All four propositions remain **Not Enough Evidence**.
