## Central question

> If artificial intelligence and automation create economic output increasingly independent of human labor, what ownership, market, community and public-capital structures allow the resulting surplus to remain broadly distributed among the people and communities whose accumulated knowledge, institutions and infrastructure helped make that productivity possible?

## Philosophical objective

> The goal is not to redistribute scarcity forever. It is to build institutions capable of distributing the benefits of abundance.

## Scarcity assumption shift

Most of HYP-131 has asked how Arkansas finances abundance while output is constrained by human labor.

AI/automation ask a different question: what happens when productive capacity grows much faster than labor required?

| Today | Automation risk |
|---|---|
| labor → production → wages → consumption → taxes | capital + AI + energy + data → production → little labor → ownership concentration |

Retraining everyone can still leave an economy that does not need historical quantities of human labor. Objective is **not** preserving unnecessary jobs — it is distributing technology’s productive benefits so abundance increases freedom rather than concentrating power.

## Dual legitimate claims

1. **Private innovators** deserve returns for genuine technology creation.  
2. **Society** has a legitimate interest in sharing extraordinary productive returns built on accumulated knowledge and publicly supported infrastructure.

**Not:** government owns AI.

## Critical variable: ownership

Prefer **owning part of the productive economy** over a giant AI tax as the primary strategy. Taxes may still capture extraordinary residual wealth; ownership creates **permanent claims on production**.

### Two futures

| Concentrated abundance | Distributed abundance |
|---|---|
| Few firms own AI/robots/energy/data/IP | Retirement, employee, co-op, community, Prosperity Fund, local enterprise, citizen vehicles |
| Labor share falls; wage tax base shrinks | AI productivity → ownership returns → household + community + Fund wealth |
| Abundance with insecurity | Closer to Constitutional Capitalism |

### Prosperity Fund pathway

Prior modeling treated Fund growth as money that must first be raised. AI abundance suggests accumulating **productive ownership before** any transition arrives — without counting projected boom as COUNTABLE NOW.

## Community AI ownership (test, don’t lock %)

Automation → higher productivity → worker/community returns + lower prices + shorter work + reinvestment — compare structures empirically.

## Work and civic time (links HYP-126)

Reject: a person has value only insofar as the market needs their labor.

Desired path: less compulsory labor → more civic time → stronger communities.  
Failure path: less employment → poverty → dependency → collapse.  
**Ownership determines which path is more likely.**

## Education join (HYP-133 — note only)

Elevate create / own / govern / adapt capabilities — not merely job training. Does **not** expand HYP-133 before Demand Map.

## Replacement Stack (hard wall)

Do **not** inflate demoted Base@16y (~$840M ceiling) for an imagined AI boom.

Add separate **AI Abundance Sensitivity** (not revenue, not COUNTABLE): compare surplus distribution under concentrated vs employee vs co-op vs Prosperity Fund vs mixed ownership.

## Hard walls

- AI abundance is a hypothesis, not a forecast  
- Projected productivity ≠ replacement-stack revenue  
- Collective inheritance ≠ government ownership of private innovation  
- Innovation incentives protected  
- Automation gains must not accrue exclusively to capital owners by default  
- Broad ownership over permanent dependency  
- Local/community retention measured, not assumed  

## Defining-idea candidate (still hypothesis)

> The economic philosophy designed for the next century cannot assume that human labor will forever remain the scarce input around which society organizes income, dignity, taxation and ownership.
