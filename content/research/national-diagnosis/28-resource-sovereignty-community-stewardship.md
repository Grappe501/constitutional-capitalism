# Resource Sovereignty and Community Stewardship

**Domain status:** priority_phase_2_architecture  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-04  
**Phase:** 2 — Diagnosis Research Foundation (central doctrine architecture; sourced modules continue in 2.1)

## Central question

Do natural resources, grid capacity, clean air, soil, and infrastructure function as free private inputs once a permit is obtained — or do they belong first to the place that bears extraction, consumption, congestion, pollution, and long-term risk, with private profit allowed only when the affected community receives a direct, permanent, measurable return?

## Why it matters

Without place-based resource return, communities can absorb water drawdown, rate pressure, truck damage, pollution, and closure liabilities while surplus leaves. Resource Sovereignty connects Stewardship, ownership, household affordability, rural prosperity, regenerative agriculture, energy resilience, and corporate accountability into one Constitutional Capitalism system.

## Strongest supporting evidence

- Scope 1/2 distinction supports total-footprint disclosure beyond the facility fence line (`CC-SRC-030`).
- Federal agencies have recognized Tribal consultation and modern mining responsibility framing (`CC-SRC-031`) — without inventing a permanent national mining statute from a press release.
- Data-center power and backup generation are recognized CAA planning issues (`CC-SRC-032`).
- USDA measurement initiatives underscore that carbon payments need monitoring credibility (`CC-SRC-035`).
- DOE defines community solar as multi-customer geographic benefit models (`CC-SRC-036`).

## Contrary evidence

- Statewide or national resource pooling can fund equalization and strategic mineral policy.
- Compact complexity can deter investment if poorly designed.
- Soil-carbon permanence, leakage, and measurement uncertainty remain contested.
- Industrial power discounts are sometimes defended as economic-development tools; net welfare effects vary.
- Local generation does not eliminate need for regional grid reliability.
- Federal GHG reporting obligations can be expanded or reduced by later administrations (`CC-SRC-033`).

## Uncertainties

- Optimal progressive-tier breakpoints are locality-specific.
- Community equity vs royalty vs fee mixes affect financeability.
- Verification protocols for a Local Carbon Restoration Exchange need sourced design.
- Microgrid evidence should not be over-attributed to the community-solar basics page alone.

## Data gaps

- No resource-sovereignty baseline metrics sourced yet.
- No invented water, electricity, emission, royalty, or carbon-credit figures.
- Legal review of property, utility, Tribal, and interstate commerce interactions remains 0%.

## Constitutional implications

Developing doctrine holds that community inherited wealth in land, water, minerals, energy capacity, air, soil, and infrastructure requires full public-cost pricing, restoration, and local return; Compacts/Ledger/Exchange are design agendas. These are **doctrine and research agenda**, not ratified Declaration amendments.

## Policy implications (not yet resolved)

Phase 2 does **not** write national royalty codes, utility tariffs, or carbon-market statutes. Future proposals must pass the Constitutional Capitalism Test and the Community Carrying-Capacity Test (`CC-RTEST-01`–`12`).
