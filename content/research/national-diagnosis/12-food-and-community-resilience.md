# Community Food Security System

**Domain status:** priority_phase_2_architecture  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-04  
**Phase:** 2 — Diagnosis Research Foundation (system architecture seeded; quantitative modules continue in 2.1)

## Central question

How can communities secure a meaningful share of essential food needs, keep more value local, reduce waste, and prepare for shocks — while remaining connected to regional, national, and international markets that stabilize supply and create opportunity?

## Why it matters

Food is critical infrastructure. Without place-based capacity, processing, reserves, and measurement, communities remain exposed to distant disruptions and often export raw products while importing finished food. Isolationist export bans are also a trap: large-scale agriculture and trade help when local harvests fail.

## Current findings

_Architecture and developing doctrine established (`CC-DEV-058`–`061`, `data/project/community_food_security_framework.json` v0.1.0)._

**Rejected principle:** “Mass production food becomes export only items.”  
**Adopted principle:** Every community should have the capacity to provide a meaningful share of its essential food needs while remaining connected to broader markets that increase resilience and economic opportunity.

## Strongest supporting evidence

- Federal definition of food security centers on reliable access to enough food for an active, healthy life (`CC-SRC-037`).
- EPA ranks preventing waste and donating food above landfill/incineration pathways (`CC-SRC-038`).
- USDA grant/program architecture explicitly supports local/regional markets and value-added products (`CC-SRC-039`).

## Contrary evidence

- Maximum specialization and distant markets can lower average costs in some commodities.
- Local capacity targets are harder in short-season or water-constrained regions.
- Food hubs and institutional procurement face viability and regulatory barriers.
- Emergency reserves can spoil or distort markets if poorly managed.
- Local-sourcing percentage metrics are definitionally difficult and easy to misuse.

## Uncertainties

- What “meaningful share” should mean by region, climate, and product class.
- Optimal mix of family-scale, mid-scale, and large-scale production inside a resilient system.
- Liability and logistics for surplus redistribution at scale.
- Dashboard indicator definitions before any values are published.

## Data gaps

- No local/regional sourcing, farmer-income, waste, or reserve metrics sourced yet (null slots only in Phase 2.1).
- No invented food-insecurity or waste percentages in this brief.

## Constitutional implications

Developing doctrine treats food as a strategic community asset under Community Food Security System architecture — Local First/Export Second as opportunity not prohibition; logistics platforms not government grocery stores. **Doctrine and research agenda**, not Declaration amendment.

## Policy implications (not yet resolved)

Phase 2 does not write procurement mandates, exchange software specs, or reserve statutes. Future proposals must pass the Community Food Security Test (`CC-FTEST-01`–`10`).

## Metrics

Candidate dashboard indicators (unsourced): local/regional food share, fresh-food access, affordability, farmer income, food waste, emergency reserve capacity, nutrition outcomes, processing capacity, new agricultural businesses.
