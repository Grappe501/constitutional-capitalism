# Wealth and Ownership

**Domain status:** priority_first_pass  
**Slice:** `CC-PHASE-2.1-THREE-LAYER-PROOF-RETROFIT-EXECUTION-1.0`  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-07  
**Phase:** 2.1 — Three-layer proof retrofit

## Central question

How is wealth and productive ownership distributed in the United States?

## Why it matters

Ownership of capital shapes who captures returns, who can weather shocks, and who can participate in economic self-government beyond wages alone.

## Current findings (sourced inventory)

Federal data show U.S. household wealth remains highly concentrated.

- **SCF 2022 (Fed bulletin, Oct 2023):** real median net worth **$192,900**; real mean **$1,063,700** — mean far above median (`CC-SRC-001`).
- **DFA / FRED shares (Q1 2026):** top 1% ≈ **28.8%**; 90–99th ≈ **34.7%**; 50–90th ≈ **31.1%**; bottom 50% ≈ **5.3%** of household wealth (`CC-SRC-002`, `CC-SRC-012`).
- An NBER working paper on the 2022 SCF reports a top-1% wealth share near **35%** under its processing choices — complementary to, not identical with, DFA quarterly shares (`CC-SRC-011`).

Between 2019 and 2022, SCF median rose faster than mean (largest three-year median increase in modern SCF history), implying some narrowing while levels of inequality remained large (`CC-SRC-001`).

## Evidence tracks

### Historical evidence

- **Established from registry:** SCF 2019→2022 shows a large median net-worth increase and some narrowing of the mean–median gap while inequality remains high (`CC-SRC-001`). DFA products describe wealth concentration since 1989 as a continuing structural pattern (`CC-SRC-002`). NBER processing of 2022 SCF notes concentration remaining high with modest decline from a mid-2010s peak under that paper’s methods (`CC-SRC-011`).
- **MISSING EVIDENCE:** No registered primary series yet attached for long-run productive-ownership / control-rights history (business equity percentiles over decades) beyond net-worth concentration.

### Economic evidence

- **Established from registry:** Levels (median/mean) from SCF (`CC-SRC-001`); percentile wealth shares from DFA/FRED (`CC-SRC-002`, `CC-SRC-012`); alternative top-1% share estimate from NBER SCF processing (`CC-SRC-011`). Baseline metrics `CC-IND-W01` and `CC-IND-W02` are the only sourced national-baseline wealth slots.
- **MISSING EVIDENCE:** Official SCF-compatible employee-ownership / business-equity shares by percentile; state/local ownership maps.

### Constitutional / legal evidence

- **MISSING EVIDENCE:** No registered constitutional or statutory sources in this domain’s source list establishing a legal rule about wealth concentration. Do not treat concentration statistics as a completed constitutional verdict.

## What the data directly establishes

1. In 2022 SCF dollars, real median household net worth was $192,900 and real mean was $1,063,700 (`CC-SRC-001`).
2. In DFA/FRED Q1 2026 share tables, the bottom 50% held about 5.3% of household wealth and the top 1% about 28.8% (`CC-SRC-002`, `CC-SRC-012`).
3. SCF methods and DFA model-based quarterly shares are not interchangeable; magnitudes differ by product (`CC-SRC-001`, `CC-SRC-002`, `CC-SRC-011`).
4. The 2019–2022 SCF episode included median rising faster than mean (`CC-SRC-001`) — concentration is not a one-way ratchet every survey cycle.

## What may reasonably be inferred

- High net-worth concentration makes ownership of capital a plausible channel for unequal shock-absorption and unequal residual claims — **inference**, not a measured control-rights map.
- Middle 40% (50–90th) still holding ~31% of wealth (`CC-SRC-012`) suggests “hollowed middle” narratives must be qualified, not absolute.
- Housing equity can lift middle-class net worth without equalizing business/financial ownership — **plausible**, not yet separated in baseline metrics.

## What Constitutional Capitalism normatively concludes

High concentration of ownership is a factual predicate for later questions about whether economic power remains accountable to a free, prosperous people. That normative agenda is **not** proven by SCF/DFA tables alone. Predictions that broader ownership would shift many households from wage-only dependence (`CC-CLAIM-010`) remain **unsupported forecasts**, not diagnosis facts.

## Contrary evidence (registered)

- 2019–2022 SCF narrowing episode (`CC-SRC-001`).
- Middle 40% still holds a substantial minority share (~31%, `CC-SRC-012`).
- Method differences (SCF levels vs DFA shares vs NBER processing) change reported extremes.

## Missing evidence (exposed)

- Financial vs housing asset percentile tables attached to baseline.
- Business equity / ESOP wealth by percentile.
- State/local ownership patterns.
- Registered constitutional/legal sources for ownership accountability doctrine.
