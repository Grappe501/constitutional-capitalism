## Central question

How can government information be presumed open and reviewable — held in trust for the people — while protecting privacy, public safety, national security, and other constitutionally or legally justified limits?

## Why it matters

Invisible bureaucracy with never-unaccountable power requires information citizens can actually inspect. Absolute disclosure is not the doctrine; secrecy-as-default is also not the doctrine. The middle path is a presumption of openness with narrow, explained, reviewable exceptions.

## Contrary evidence

- Overbroad publication can expose personal data, investigative methods, or security vulnerabilities.
- Unified ledgers are costly and can create single points of failure.
- Mandatory identity for routine viewing can chill journalism and whistleblowing.
- AI summaries can misstate records if not citation-bound.

## Uncertainties

- Measurement of dollar-trail completeness across fragmented systems.

## Constitutional implications

Developing doctrine: information held in trust; presumed open; government bears withholding burden; People's Ledger digital trails; equal access without chilling ID rules; AI interprets, records remain official. Not Declaration amendments.
