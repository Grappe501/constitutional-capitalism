## Central question

How do we build a government that delivers better outcomes with fewer layers, less friction, and greater accountability — without hollowing out capacity or expanding unaccountable bureaucracy?

## Why it matters

Most political debates ask only “more government or less government.” Justice and democracy spines fail in practice if agencies, boards, and service systems are opaque, captured, digitally exclusive, or administratively exhausting. Public service is stewardship of a public trust.

## Contrary evidence

- Aggressive headcount cuts can destroy institutional knowledge and service quality.
- Digital-first services can exclude elderly, disabled, rural, poor, and offline residents without human pathways.
- Some administrative friction is legitimate oversight; simplification can weaken safeguards.
- Interagency data reuse without strong authorization can violate privacy.
- Sunset reviews and credentials can themselves be captured or become barriers to entry.
- Performance dashboards can become public-relations theater if failures are omitted.
- Human-in-the-loop AI can become rubber-stamping without real review capacity.

## Uncertainties

- Optimal civil-service vs at-will mixes by function.
- Measurement of citizen-experience metrics across life-event journeys.
- Legal boundaries for collect-once reuse and AI-assisted adjudication.
- Scope of quasi-public entities in a complete authority inventory.

## Constitutional implications

Developing doctrine: reduce friction while improving service; invisible bureaucracy with never-unaccountable power; automate routine / elevate human; collect once with legal authorization; AI never final on rights/significant benefits; visible boards; sunset/renewal. Not Declaration amendments.
