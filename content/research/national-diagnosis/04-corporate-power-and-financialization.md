## Central question

How concentrated is corporate economic power, and what is known vs unknown about financialization?

## Why it matters

Corporate scale and financial claims can reshape labor markets, local economies, and political influence.

## Current findings (sourced inventory)

The Census Bureau’s Economic Census concentration tables (`CC-SRC-005`, EC2200SIZECONCEN) provide official industry-level concentration ratios (e.g., CR4/CR8 and related largest-firm shares). These establish a **descriptive** evidence base that many industries have substantial large-firm shares.

`CC-CLAIM-005` (concentration can weaken competition) is **partially_supported**: data show concentration; competitive effects on prices/wages/entry are industry-specific and debated.

Financialization (rising role of financial motives, markets, and actors) remains a **research queue** in this slice — architecture present, **no invented macro financialization index** asserted.

## What the data directly establishes

1. Official Economic Census concentration products report largest-firm shares within industries for 2022 (`CC-SRC-005`).
2. Those products enable tracking of concentration ratios; they do **not** by themselves prove monopoly power, consumer harm, or political capture.
3. No registered source in this domain yet establishes a national “financialization index” for baseline use.

## What may reasonably be inferred

- Large-firm shares in many industries create a factual backdrop for competition and power analysis — **inference** from descriptive concentration (`CC-SRC-005`).
- Rising concentration can reflect efficiency/scale as well as weakened rivalry — **both readings remain open** (see contrary evidence).
- Financialization as a macro diagnosis is **plausible research agenda**, not documented here (`untested` until sources register).

## What Constitutional Capitalism normatively concludes

Economic power concentration is a factual input to later accountability design — not proof of capture by itself (`CC-CLAIM-003` remains open). Normative anti-entrenchment goals must wait for sourced competition, political-economy, and legal modules.

## Contrary evidence (registered)

- Rising concentration can reflect efficiency and scale economies.
- National concentration can misstate local or digital rivalry.
- Not all concentrated industries show elevated markups or wage suppression.
