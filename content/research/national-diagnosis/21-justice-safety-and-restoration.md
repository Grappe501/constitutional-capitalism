## Central question

Does the justice system protect the public, restrain wrongdoing, repair harm, and return people to productive citizenship when safe — or does it function as a profit center, political weapon, or permanent system of social exclusion that makes liberty and ownership conditional on wealth?

## Why it matters

Justice is the mechanism that determines whether liberty, ownership, opportunity, and accountability are real—or merely promised. Economic diagnosis alone cannot answer whether power returns to the people if the justice system can destroy freedom, family, property, employment, housing, voting power, or future through poverty, unaffordable counsel, fees, or lifetime exclusion.

## Contrary evidence

- Public-safety and incapacitation literatures argue some offenses require secure confinement; reform that ignores violence or predation can harm victims and communities.
- Restorative processes are not appropriate or safe in every case; victim participation must remain voluntary.
- Measurement of “rehabilitation success” and recidivism is contested (definitions, follow-up windows, selection).
- Local control of policing can improve accountability **or** enable rights violations without constitutional floors.

## Uncertainties

- Causal identification of cash-bail and public-defense funding effects varies by jurisdiction and study design.
- Optimal mix of prevention, diversion, incarceration, and supervision is offense- and community-specific.
- Record-sealing / reentry policy effects on employment and recidivism need careful sourced review.

## Constitutional implications

Developing doctrine holds that equal justice cannot depend primarily on wealth; punishment must be proportionate; institutions must not profit from punishment; victims must be supported; and reentry into citizenship should be the default after lawful completion of sentence, with risk-specific reviewable exceptions. These are **doctrine and research agenda**, not ratified Declaration amendments.

## Policy implications (not yet resolved)

Phase 2 does **not** write national criminal codes, sentencing grids, or policing statutes. Future proposals must pass both the Constitutional Capitalism Test and the Justice Test (`CC-JTEST-01`–`10`).
