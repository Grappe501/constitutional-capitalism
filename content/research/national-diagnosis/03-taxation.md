## Central question

How do federal taxes and transfers currently distribute income, and what remains unknown about incidence?

## Why it matters

Tax design is a core lever in any economic constitution; diagnosis must separate distributional facts from normative redesign.

## Current findings (sourced inventory)

CBO’s *Distribution of Household Income in 2021* (`CC-SRC-013`) shows large pre-tax income gaps: average income before transfers and taxes in the highest quintile was roughly **19×** the lowest quintile; after means-tested transfers and federal taxes, about **7×**. Transfers and taxes therefore compress inequality substantially without eliminating it. 2021 includes pandemic-era credits and elevated capital gains — year effects matter.

`CC-CLAIM-013` (payroll/income taxes as major labor-income burden components) is **partially_supported** at the institutional/distributional level via CBO; detailed payroll-vs-income incidence modules remain open.

`CC-CLAIM-008` (destination-based taxation) and `CC-CLAIM-014` (corporate-tax incidence on workers/consumers) remain **requires_additional_research** — not treated as facts.

## What the data directly establishes

1. In CBO’s 2021 household distribution, highest-quintile average income before transfers and taxes was about 19× the lowest quintile; after means-tested transfers and federal taxes, about 7× (`CC-SRC-013`).
2. Federal transfers and taxes compressed measured income inequality in 2021 without eliminating it (`CC-SRC-013`).
3. 2021 is not a steady-state year (pandemic-era credits; elevated capital gains) (`CC-SRC-013` limitations / brief caveats).
4. Destination-based taxation and corporate-tax incidence are **not** established by `CC-SRC-013`.

## What may reasonably be inferred

- The federal fiscal system currently performs substantial redistribution relative to market income — **inference** from one CBO year, not a complete tax-architecture map.
- Year effects mean 2021 ratios can overstate or understate “normal” compression — **inference**.
- Statutory vs economic incidence may diverge for corporate and property taxes — **open**, not settled.

## What Constitutional Capitalism normatively concludes

Tax power is part of any serious economic constitution. Distributional facts motivate later design under the Constitutional Capitalism Test; they do **not** enact a tax code, approve destination-based taxation, or settle incidence debates. Normative redesign must wait for sourced incidence and legal review.
