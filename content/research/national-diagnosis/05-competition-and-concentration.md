## Central question

What do official concentration statistics show, and what do they not prove?

## Why it matters

Competition policy depends on measurement that is honest about limits.

**Plain English:** Enforcement activity is useful; 20 criminal cases is **not** a measure of market competitiveness. Entry counts answer a different question. Panel: `CC-EP-MARKET-DYNAMICS-SYSTEM-1`.

## Current findings (sourced inventory)

Census Economic Census concentration products (`CC-SRC-005`) are the primary official spine. They report largest-firm shares within industries; they do **not** by themselves prove monopoly power, consumer harm, or political capture.

This brief shares evidence with the corporate-power domain; competition is treated as the measurement layer, power as the broader interpretation layer.

## What the data directly establishes

1. Census concentration products report largest-firm shares within defined industries (`CC-SRC-005`).
2. Concentration ratios are measurement outputs, not automatic findings of consumer harm or monopoly.
3. Product-market definition choices can change apparent concentration — a measurement limit acknowledged in the claim ledger opposing notes for `CC-CLAIM-005`.

## What may reasonably be inferred

- Official CR data are a necessary spine for competition diagnosis — **inference** about research method, not a policy conclusion.
- Entry, imports, and multi-sided platforms may discipline concentrated sellers in some markets — **plausible contrary mechanism**, not yet quantified here.
- Platform ecosystems spanning NAICS codes may understate or overstate rivalry — **open**.

## What Constitutional Capitalism normatively concludes

Competition policy implicates both commerce powers and liberty interests. Honest diagnosis refuses to treat CR4 as a constitutional verdict. No merger guidelines are rewritten in this brief.
