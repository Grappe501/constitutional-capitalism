## Central research question

> Can a rural public school district become a regional education, agriculture, and skilled-trades hub that attracts students, strengthens the local economy, supports family farms, and creates a sustainable pathway for community growth?

Working concept (unproven): grades **9–12**, regional transportation, student housing only in carefully governed stages after demand and capacity are demonstrated.

## First major research gate — student funding

Before recruitment or housing design, determine how additional students affect district revenue and obligations.

Distinguish enrollment pathways and funding streams (transfers, school choice, specialized programs with home-district enrollment, temporary residence, interdistrict agreements, residential students, CTE, weighted/categorical, transportation, facilities, special education, federal eligibility).

Critical calculation:

> **Marginal recurring revenue per additional student − full marginal cost** (instruction, transportation, housing, food, staffing, student services, facilities, compliance).

Enrollment growth can weaken finances if responsibilities exceed revenue.

## USDA and other funding (tools, not presumed sources)

Map eligibility, allowable use, match, renewal, timing, operations vs capital — for rural community facilities, distance learning, business/cooperative development, food systems, farm-to-school, value-added ag, beginning farmers, housing, utilities, broadband, energy, workforce, plus federal/state education, community college, apprenticeships, philanthropy, employer training, veteran pathways, public-private infrastructure.

## Regional transportation

Central, not incidental: travel radius, routes, times, fleet/drivers, extracurricular/trade schedules, weather, reimbursement, hub-and-spoke vs household routes. A regional school fails if travel consumes an unreasonable share of the day.

## Housing as graduated stages

1. Regional commuting (no residential)  
2. Limited weekly housing (distance/schedule)  
3. Education village (purpose-built + safeguarding)  
4. Community growth (teachers, instructors, apprentices, young farm families, healthcare, partners)  

Each stage requires demonstrated demand, financial sustainability, safeguarding capacity, and community approval.

## Farm-community connection

Surrounding community as learning environment — family farms, livestock, regenerative sites, food hubs, markets, equipment/repair, veterinary, conservation, cooperatives, rural healthcare, EMS, local government — school as anchor that helps existing farms/businesses grow.

## Impacts and harms to study

Enrollment/finances, employment, instructor recruitment, housing, roads/utilities, EMS, healthcare, farm succession, business formation, childcare, property values/affordability, identity, civic participation, population retention — plus financial overextension, displacement/inflation, taxpayer burden, transport inequity, mission drift, student-safety risks, grant dependence, and drawing students/resources from neighboring districts.

## Scenarios (later)

1. Existing trajectory  
2. Regional career academy (9–12 + transportation)  
3. Rural education and trade hub (+ dual credit, apprenticeships, farm partnerships)  
4. Residential rural innovation center (housing only after earlier stages prove sustainable)  

Compare enrollment, revenue, operating cost, capital, staffing, transportation, economic effects, risks, legal complexity. Scenario estimates — never forecasts.
