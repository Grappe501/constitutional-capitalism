## 1. Status / fairness disclaimer

This dossier is a comparative instrument for Constitutional Capitalism. Categories overlap; real societies are usually hybrids. Pejorative or diagnostic labels (where used) describe patterns of power, not identities people must proudly claim.

**Phase 2 status:** Wealth (Z.1/DFA/SCF family), ownership, and political-money panels are live-bound. Measurable concentration does **not** prove a completed plutocracy regime.

## 2. Definition

Plutocracy names political systems—or tendencies within democracies—where wealth confers decisive political power beyond formal one-person-one-vote equality.

**Plain version:** Plutocracy is analyzed here as a political-economic pattern with distinctive ownership, market, and authority arrangements—not as a single timeless nation.

## 3. How it functions

Campaign spending, lobbying, revolving doors, and agenda control skew policy. Markets may be competitive yet politics unequal.

| Dimension | Summary |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Wealth holders dominate ownership and politics |
| Property regime | Strong for elites |
| Markets | Markets reinforce wealth-to-power translation |
| Government / polity | Disproportionately responsive to wealth |
| Labor / work | Labor voice diminished relative to capital |
| Power & concentration | Money translates into political control |
| Accountability | Campaign finance, lobbying, and elite media influence (mechanisms vary) |

## 4. Why it emerges / what sustains it

Costly campaigns, organizational advantages of concentrated wealth, and weak countervailing civic institutions.

## 5. What informed adherents or beneficiaries claim

Beneficiaries claim that wealth signals success and that capital needs political protection to invest. Democratic theorists treat this as a corruption of equality.

These are **reported claims**, not project endorsements.

**Strengths often cited:** Investment capacity; Policy attention to capital formation.

## 6. Major weaknesses and failure modes

Unequal responsiveness, policy bias, and legitimacy crises. Empirical magnitudes of influence are researched case-by-case—not invented here.

**Risks tagged in the matrix:** Political inequality; Capture; Erosion of equal citizenship.

## 7. Historical conditions and exemplars

Diagnostic tendency measurable in degrees; rarely a self-ascribed constitution.

**Exemplars / references (non-statistical):** Progressive Era critiques; Contemporary money-in-politics research programs.
