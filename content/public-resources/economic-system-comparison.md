# Economic System Comparison

> **Developing analytical tool — not settled scholarship.** This comparison is a working aid, not a definitive account of any tradition. Real-world systems are usually hybrids. Descriptions aim for fairness to informed adherents without endorsing every system. **No invented statistics.**

Constitutional Capitalism is a developing framework, not a settled ideology. It protects private property and enterprise while asking how constitutional law can prevent both public and private domination.

## Historical

### [Feudalism](/compare/feudalism/)
**Ownership:** Layered land tenure under lords; peasants hold use-rights under obligation

**Markets:** Local and limited; land and labor often non-market or weakly marketized

**Government:** Fragmented lordship and personal jurisdiction more than modern territorial state

**Property regime:** Conditional tenure, not modern absolute title

**Labor:** Customary dues, labor services, serfdom or dependent peasantry in many settings

**Concentrated power:** Hierarchy of military-landholding elites

**Accountability:** Custom, oath, and local force rather than impersonal public law

**Historical exemplars:** High medieval western Europe (ideal-type); Regional seigneurial regimes

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Historical contrast: CC requires impersonal constitutional property and citizenship, not personal dependence

**Strengths:** Local order under weak central capacity; Reciprocal (if unequal) protection bargains. **Risks:** Arbitrary lordship; Immobility; Violence and fragmented justice.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/feudalism.md`

### [Guild / medieval corporate economy](/compare/guild-medieval-economy/)
**Ownership:** Artisan and merchant corporate ownership within town privileges

**Markets:** Regulated markets; entry, quality, and prices often guild-governed

**Government:** Municipal and corporate privileges bargain with princes and towns

**Property regime:** Strong for members within corporate rules; outsiders restricted

**Labor:** Apprenticeship and journeyman hierarchies; mastership as gate

**Concentrated power:** Corporate monopoly privileges by craft and town

**Accountability:** Guild statutes, municipal courts, custom

**Historical exemplars:** Late medieval Italian and Northern European towns; Craft guild charters

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC may respect associations and skill standards but rejects closed privilege as a constitutional baseline

**Strengths:** Quality control; Skill transmission; Member mutual aid. **Risks:** Entry barriers; Innovation drag; Insider privilege.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/guild-medieval-economy.md`

### [Physiocracy](/compare/physiocracy/)
**Ownership:** Private landed property as the productive core

**Markets:** Free grain trade and natural price formation emphasized

**Government:** Legal despotism in physiocratic theory: strong authority to secure natural order

**Property regime:** Strong, especially agricultural land

**Labor:** Agriculture privileged as productive class; industry often labeled sterile in theory

**Concentrated power:** Landed surplus and single tax on net product as fiscal redesign

**Accountability:** Natural law and enlightened administration

**Historical exemplars:** Quesnay's Tableau économique; Turgot-era reform debates

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Shares anti-privilege and property themes; CC rejects physiocratic class metaphysics and legal despotism

**Strengths:** Early systematic political economy; Critique of mercantilist privilege. **Risks:** Agricultural monism; Authoritarian legal-despotism risk; Undervaluation of industry.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/physiocracy.md`

### [Ancient tributary / palace economy](/compare/tributary-palace-economy/)
**Ownership:** Palace, temple, or imperial centers claim superior claims on surplus

**Markets:** Markets exist at margins; allocation often administered by redistributive centers

**Government:** Ruler-centered extraction and redistribution; household of the palace as economic hub

**Property regime:** Conditional and stratified; elite and institutional holdings dominate

**Labor:** Corvée, dependent labor, slavery, and tribute obligations common in variants

**Concentrated power:** Central ceremonial-political concentration of surplus

**Accountability:** Divine kingship, temple authority, imperial hierarchy

**Historical exemplars:** Mycenaean palace records (ideal-type reference); Ancient Near Eastern redistributive polities (comparative)

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary ideal-type: CC rejects palace-centered domination and requires constitutional limits on extractive power

**Strengths:** Large-scale mobilization; Grain storage and crisis redistribution capacity. **Risks:** Extractive predation; Information and incentive failures; Subject vulnerability.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/tributary-palace-economy.md`

### [Classical republican mixed economy](/compare/classical-republican-mixed-economy/)
**Ownership:** Private household property with civic limits against luxury and dependency

**Markets:** Commerce accepted with suspicion of corruption and dependency

**Government:** Mixed constitution; civic virtue and anti-domination as political aims

**Property regime:** Strong for citizens; tied to independence and military/civic capacity

**Labor:** Ideal of independent proprietor; historical reliance on slavery or dependents in many republics

**Concentrated power:** Agrarian laws, sumptuary norms, and mixed offices to check oligarchy

**Accountability:** Civic virtue, mixed institutions, public contestation

**Historical exemplars:** Roman republican ideology (idealized); Machiavelli and Atlantic republican discourse; Early American agrarian republican themes

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Major intellectual neighbor on anti-domination and broad independent ownership; CC must reject historical exclusions while learning the independence-property link

**Strengths:** Anti-domination ethic; Link of property to citizenship; Suspicion of corrupting concentration. **Risks:** Exclusionary citizenship; Slavery and patriarchy in historical republics; Anti-commercial romanticism.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/classical-republican-mixed-economy.md`

### [Mercantilism](/compare/mercantilism/)
**Ownership:** Mixed private ownership under national strategic direction

**Markets:** Trade managed for national advantage; bullion and balance-of-trade focus historically

**Government:** Uses tariffs, monopolies, navigation acts, and charters

**Property regime:** Conditional on sovereign strategy and privilege

**Labor:** Labor serves national production and naval/military capacity

**Concentrated power:** Chartered monopolies and favored companies common

**Accountability:** Sovereign strategy and fiscal-military state

**Historical exemplars:** Colbertist France; English Navigation Acts era; Chartered companies

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects privilege charters while allowing lawful resilience and competition policy — not mercantile monopoly

**Strengths:** State capacity building; Infant-industry and naval power logics. **Risks:** Rent seeking; War and commercial conflict; Consumer costs of privilege.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/mercantilism.md`

## Market-capitalist

### [Laissez-faire capitalism](/compare/laissez-faire-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Predominantly private ownership

**Markets:** Markets coordinate most activity with minimal intervention

**Government:** Limited to property, contract, basic order, and narrow public goods

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Contractual wage labor

**Concentrated power:** Relies largely on entry and market correction

**Accountability:** Private contract and market discipline

**Historical exemplars:** Classical liberal treatises; Select 19th-century policy regimes (approximate)

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Partial ancestor; CC adds constitutional accountability for public and private domination

**Strengths:** Entrepreneurship; Price discovery; Low discretionary intervention. **Risks:** Externalities; Concentrated private power; Under-provision of public goods.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/laissez-faire-capitalism.md`

### [Shareholder-primacy capitalism](/compare/shareholder-primacy-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Private corporate ownership oriented to residual shareholder claims

**Markets:** Capital and control markets discipline managers

**Government:** Sets corporate, securities, and bankruptcy law

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Labor treated chiefly as contractual input

**Concentrated power:** Competition law plus capital-market discipline

**Accountability:** Shareholder value metrics and fiduciary doctrines (as interpreted)

**Historical exemplars:** Berle-Means corporation debates; Late-20th-century agency theory influence

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects shareholder value as the sole constitutional lens on corporate power

**Strengths:** Clear managerial objective; Capital formation; Exit discipline via markets. **Risks:** Short-termism; Stakeholder externalities; Agency problems despite the slogan.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/shareholder-primacy-capitalism.md`

### [Neoliberalism](/compare/neoliberalism/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership expanded via privatization and marketization

**Markets:** Markets actively constructed and extended into new domains

**Government:** Strong state as market-maker: competition rules, monetary regimes, globalization architecture

**Property regime:** Strong; intellectual property and finance emphasized

**Labor:** Flexible labor markets; unions often weakened relative to mid-century peaks

**Concentrated power:** Competition policy varies; finance and platform concentration recurring issues

**Accountability:** Price signals, independent agencies, and international economic rules

**Historical exemplars:** Post-1970s policy shifts in multiple OECD countries; Washington Consensus debates

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Overlaps on markets and property; CC rejects reducing constitutional order to market efficiency alone

**Strengths:** Anti-inflation monetary credibility (claimed); Trade integration; Entrepreneurial dynamism. **Risks:** Financial instability; Inequality; Democratic backlash; Label overuse.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/neoliberalism.md`

### [Keynesian / managed capitalism](/compare/keynesian-managed-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Mostly private ownership with periodic public enterprise

**Markets:** Markets central; demand management and stabilization overlay

**Government:** Fiscal-monetary stabilization, automatic stabilizers, sometimes incomes policy

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Full employment as a political goal; union bargaining often institutionalized

**Concentrated power:** Competition policy plus macroeconomic management; not primarily ownership socialization

**Accountability:** Electoral democracy and technocratic economic management

**Historical exemplars:** Postwar OECD 'golden age' debates; Keynesian policy manuals

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Compatible toolkit elements possible; CC is not identical to mid-century managed capitalism

**Strengths:** Stabilization ambition; Employment focus; Crisis response toolkit. **Risks:** Inflation; Fiscal stress; Political business cycles; Capture of industrial policy.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/keynesian-managed-capitalism.md`

### [Welfare capitalism](/compare/welfare-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership with employer or public benefits layered on

**Markets:** Markets central for production and jobs

**Government:** Supports income, health, and social insurance—or mandates employer provision

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Benefits supplement wages; employment often gateway to security

**Concentrated power:** Conventional competition policy; social insurance as counterweight

**Accountability:** Employers, insurers, and welfare-state institutions

**Historical exemplars:** Company welfare traditions; Bismarckian and Beveridgean insurance lineages

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC may incorporate compatible protections without equating CC to welfare capitalism

**Strengths:** Security within markets; Political stabilization of capitalism. **Risks:** Employer dependency; Coverage gaps; Cost and fiscal pressures.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/welfare-capitalism.md`

### [Stakeholder capitalism](/compare/stakeholder-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership with multiple stakeholder duties claimed

**Markets:** Markets central

**Government:** Sets disclosure, fiduciary expectations, and soft-law codes

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Labor recognized as stakeholder alongside communities and environment

**Concentrated power:** Voluntary or legal stakeholder governance; enforcement often soft

**Accountability:** Corporate governance codes, ESG metrics, reputation

**Historical exemplars:** Business Roundtable 2019 statement debates; Rhenish governance comparisons

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC seeks constitutional grounding for accountability rather than voluntary stakeholder rhetoric alone

**Strengths:** Broader impact recognition; Managerial ethical language. **Risks:** Vagueness; Weak enforcement; Managerial unaccountability.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/stakeholder-capitalism.md`

### [Public-benefit capitalism](/compare/public-benefit-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership including mission-locked or benefit entities

**Markets:** Markets with social-purpose corporate forms

**Government:** Authorizes and regulates benefit corporations and similar forms

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Labor treated as stakeholder within mission governance

**Concentrated power:** Voluntary mission governance; not a full concentration remedy

**Accountability:** Charters, benefit reports, and limited fiduciary redesign

**Historical exemplars:** U.S. benefit corporation statutes; Community interest company analogues abroad

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Compatible mechanism family, not a complete CC system

**Strengths:** Mission protection; Legal clarity vs pure soft stakeholderism. **Risks:** Voluntary uptake; Enforcement thinness; Greenwashing risk.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/public-benefit-capitalism.md`

### [Anarcho-capitalism](/compare/anarcho-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Fully private ownership including security and law as market goods

**Markets:** Markets replace state provision of nearly all services

**Government:** Abolished or reduced to vanishing point in the ideal

**Property regime:** Maximal

**Labor:** Pure contract; no public labor regime

**Concentrated power:** Assumes competition among private defense agencies prevents domination

**Accountability:** Reputation, contract, and exit among private providers

**Historical exemplars:** Theoretical literature (Rothbard, Friedman); No durable large-scale national exemplar

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC affirms limited capable constitutional government; rejects abolition of the public coercive framework

**Strengths:** Radical anti-coercion ethic (as claimed); Consistency about monopoly of force skepticism. **Risks:** Warlord and cartel risk; Public-goods underprovision; Unequal access to justice.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/anarcho-capitalism.md`

### [Mixed economy](/compare/mixed-economy/)
**Ownership:** Private enterprise dominant with public ownership in selected sectors

**Markets:** Markets allocate most goods; planning or regulation in others

**Government:** Regulator, insurer, sometimes producer; shares vary by country

**Property regime:** Strong but bounded by regulation and public domains

**Labor:** Wage labor with public labor law overlays

**Concentrated power:** Competition law, regulation, and occasional public options

**Accountability:** Democratic politics plus market discipline

**Historical exemplars:** Postwar Western economies; Contemporary OECD mixes

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Describes many baselines CC would reform; mixed economy is a description, not CC's distinctive constitutional thesis

**Strengths:** Pragmatic hybridity; Sector-specific tools; Empirical commonality. **Risks:** Conceptual vagueness; Inconsistent accountability; Fiscal and regulatory overload.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/mixed-economy.md`

### [Constitutional Capitalism](/compare/constitutional-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Secure private and broadly distributed ownership

**Markets:** Free and fair markets under constitutional safeguards

**Government:** Limited, capable, democratically accountable rule enforcement

**Property regime:** Strong, subject to lawful obligations

**Labor:** Workers possess dignity, voice, and optional ownership pathways

**Concentrated power:** Prevents domination by public or private power

**Accountability:** Constitutional law, democratic accountability, and contestable markets

**Historical exemplars:** Project Declaration and principles (in development); Comparative lessons from ordoliberal and republican traditions

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** The developing framework being defined — not settled doctrine completion

**Strengths:** Liberty; Pluralism; Broad opportunity; Anti-domination aim. **Risks:** Unresolved mechanisms; Overbreadth risk; Implementation incompleteness.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/constitutional-capitalism.md`

## Coordinated-market

### [Social-market economics](/compare/social-market-economics/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership within a social legal order

**Markets:** Markets coordinate under social safeguards

**Government:** Maintains competition and social protection

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Labor protections and social partnership

**Concentrated power:** Strong competition policy

**Accountability:** Constitutional democratic law

**Historical exemplars:** Soziale Marktwirtschaft discourse; Postwar Federal Republic economic order debates

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Close comparative reference, not an identity

**Strengths:** Competitive dynamism with social security. **Risks:** Complexity; Fiscal demands; Corporatist insider risk.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/social-market-economics.md`

### [Ordoliberalism](/compare/ordoliberalism/)
**Ownership:** Private ownership under a competitive order

**Markets:** Markets require an ordering legal framework to remain free

**Government:** Strong rule-setting state; limited discretionary industrial direction

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Labor protected within legal order

**Concentrated power:** Prevents monopolies and privileges as constitutional-economic duty

**Accountability:** Rule of law and competition order

**Historical exemplars:** Freiburg School texts; German competition-law tradition influences

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Major intellectual comparison for CC's competition and anti-privilege aims

**Strengths:** Anti-monopoly clarity; Order-based liberalism. **Risks:** May understate distributional conflict; Technocratic rigidity risk.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/ordoliberalism.md`

### [Social democracy](/compare/social-democracy/)
**Ownership:** Mostly private ownership with strong public welfare and regulation

**Markets:** Markets remain central for production

**Government:** Provides services, redistribution, and labor protections

**Property regime:** Strong

**Labor:** Strong labor protections and collective bargaining

**Concentrated power:** Competition law plus welfare and labor counterweights

**Accountability:** Democratic institutions and parties

**Historical exemplars:** Nordic model debates; Postwar European social democratic governments

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Overlaps on democratic accountability; CC remains distinct and not identical

**Strengths:** Security; Equality of opportunity ambitions; Mass legitimacy. **Risks:** Fiscal burden; Administrative complexity; Globalization constraints.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/social-democracy.md`

### [Corporatism](/compare/corporatism/)
**Ownership:** Private or mixed ownership organized by functional groups

**Markets:** Markets coordinated through organized interests

**Government:** Bargains with and directs recognized groups

**Property regime:** Variable

**Labor:** Labor represented through official peak associations

**Concentrated power:** Managed through group bargaining

**Accountability:** State-recognized associations

**Historical exemplars:** Postwar Austrian/Scandinavian neocorporatism; Interwar authoritarian corporatist ideologies (distinct)

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC may learn from coordination but rejects closed privilege and compulsory functional representation as constitutional baseline

**Strengths:** Coordination; Peak-level representation; Strike reduction potential. **Risks:** Exclusion of outsiders; Elite bargaining opacity; Authoritarian variants.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/corporatism.md`

### [Developmental state](/compare/developmental-state/)
**Ownership:** Private firms guided by selective state industrial policy

**Markets:** Export markets central; domestic markets often steered

**Government:** Pilot agencies, credit steering, performance discipline

**Property regime:** Strong but politically conditional in strategic sectors

**Labor:** Varies; often repression or incorporation during high-growth phases

**Concentrated power:** National champions and conglomerates cultivated then sometimes disciplined

**Accountability:** Performance legitimacy and bureaucratic insulation (contested)

**Historical exemplars:** Japan MITI debates; South Korea and Taiwan high-growth literatures

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects authoritarian insulation; may study performance discipline without importing unaccountable pilot agencies

**Strengths:** Rapid industrialization capacity; Learning-by-doing coordination. **Risks:** Authoritarian politics; Crony degeneration; Hard mid-income transitions.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/developmental-state.md`

### [Distributism](/compare/distributism/)
**Ownership:** Widely distributed small-scale productive property

**Markets:** Markets with preference for small owners and local exchange

**Government:** Supports ownership dispersion and associations; anti-monopoly spirit

**Property regime:** Strong and broad

**Labor:** Family and guild-oriented work; skepticism of proletarian wage dependence

**Concentrated power:** Structural dispersion of property

**Accountability:** Household, parish, and local association

**Historical exemplars:** Chesterton and Belloc writings; Select cooperative/agrarian experiments

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Shares broad ownership aim; not a fixed CC form or confessional program

**Strengths:** Independence; Local ownership; Anti-servility ethic. **Risks:** Scale limitations; Romanticism; Under-specified macro institutions.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/distributism.md`

### [Cooperative economics](/compare/cooperative-economics/)
**Ownership:** Member-owned firms and associations

**Markets:** Markets can coordinate cooperatives alongside other firms

**Government:** Enables cooperative legal structures and sometimes preferential policy

**Property regime:** Strong, often collective among members

**Labor:** Workers or consumer-members govern enterprises

**Concentrated power:** Member ownership as counter to investor concentration

**Accountability:** Member governance (one-member-one-vote norms common)

**Historical exemplars:** Rochdale principles; Mondragon (comparative case literature); Credit union movements

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC treats cooperatives as one possible ownership mechanism among others

**Strengths:** Participation; Shared returns; Local anchoring potential. **Risks:** Capital access constraints; Governance collective-action problems.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/cooperative-economics.md`

### [Commons / common-pool resource governance](/compare/commons-cpr-governance/)
**Ownership:** Shared resource systems with community rules (not open-access chaos)

**Markets:** May coexist; core allocation often by local rules and norms

**Government:** May recognize, support, or undermine local governance

**Property regime:** Nested rights; not simple private vs state binary

**Labor:** Users contribute monitoring and maintenance labor

**Concentrated power:** Local rule design to prevent overuse and elite capture (aspirational)

**Accountability:** User communities and nested institutions

**Historical exemplars:** Irrigation communities; Fisheries and forest commons case studies in Ostrom tradition

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Informative for polycentric governance; not a complete national economic system substitute for CC

**Strengths:** Local knowledge; Sustainable use potential; Polycentric governance insights. **Risks:** Elite capture; Scale limits; External pressure breakdown.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/commons-cpr-governance.md`

### [Georgism](/compare/georgism/)
**Ownership:** Private improvements; social claim on land rents

**Markets:** Markets for goods and labor; land taxed to capture unearned rent

**Government:** Assesses and taxes land value; otherwise often market-friendly

**Property regime:** Strong in produced capital; land rent socialized via tax

**Labor:** Labor and capital keep what they produce; land rent redirected

**Concentrated power:** Attacks land monopoly and speculative rent

**Accountability:** Fiscal transparency of land assessments

**Historical exemplars:** Henry George's Progress and Poverty movement; Select land-value tax local experiments

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Interesting anti-rent instrument set; not identical to CC and not silently adopted as doctrine

**Strengths:** Clear rent theory; Anti-speculation logic; Compatibility with markets. **Risks:** Assessment politics; Transition conflicts; Incomplete as full system.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/georgism.md`

## Socialist

### [Democratic socialism](/compare/democratic-socialism/)
**Ownership:** Substantial social, public, or worker ownership of major assets

**Markets:** Markets vary by program—from limited to sectoral

**Government:** Democratic direction of investment and ownership forms

**Property regime:** Limited for major productive assets

**Labor:** Labor as co-owner or democratic participant

**Concentrated power:** Public/social ownership as primary curb on capital concentration

**Accountability:** Democratic social control

**Historical exemplars:** Programmatic party platforms; Select nationalization waves under democratic governments

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC protects private property as foundational and is not this system

**Strengths:** Equality ambitions; Workplace voice; Public control of investment. **Risks:** State or collective control risks; Capital flight; Innovation and calculation debates.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/democratic-socialism.md`

### [Socialism](/compare/socialism/)
**Ownership:** Social, cooperative, or state ownership of major productive assets

**Markets:** Markets range from limited to absent across variants

**Government:** Directs or owns significant production

**Property regime:** Restricted for major assets

**Labor:** Labor framed as collective producer

**Concentrated power:** Social ownership as primary remedy to capital concentration

**Accountability:** Social ownership or planning institutions

**Historical exemplars:** Diverse national experiments and movements; Second International traditions

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC is not socialism

**Strengths:** Equality aims; Coordination ambitions; Decommodification goals. **Risks:** Liberty risks; Innovation and incentive problems; New elite formation.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/socialism.md`

### [Marxism](/compare/marxism/)
**Ownership:** Collective ownership after class transformation

**Markets:** Markets seen as historically superseded under full communism; socialism as transition debated

**Government:** State often transitional in theory (dictatorship of the proletariat debates)

**Property regime:** Abolished for productive capital

**Labor:** Labor emancipated from wage relation in the ideal trajectory

**Concentrated power:** Class abolition through revolutionary transformation

**Accountability:** Historical materialism and proletarian political rule (theory)

**Historical exemplars:** Marx and Engels theoretical corpus; Diverse 20th-century Marxist parties and states

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects class-revolutionary premises and abolition of productive private property

**Strengths:** Structural critique of exploitation and crisis; Totalizing social analysis. **Risks:** Authoritarian implementation risks; Teleology; Suppression of pluralism.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/marxism.md`

### [Communism](/compare/communism/)
**Ownership:** Common ownership in a classless ideal

**Markets:** Markets abolished in the ideal form

**Government:** State withers away in theory; party-states claimed the name in practice

**Property regime:** Abolished for productive assets in the ideal

**Labor:** Labor allocated by communal principle ('from each…')

**Concentrated power:** Eliminates class ownership in theory

**Accountability:** Communal self-rule in theory; party hierarchy in many practices

**Historical exemplars:** Marxian end-state writings; 20th-century Communist Party states (practical claimants)

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects abolition of property and markets

**Strengths:** Egalitarian ideal; Critique of class division. **Risks:** Coordination problems; Coercion in practice; Gap between ideal and party-state.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/communism.md`

### [Command economies](/compare/command-economies/)
**Ownership:** State ownership or command over production

**Markets:** Planning replaces most market allocation

**Government:** Central planners direct output, prices, and investment

**Property regime:** Restricted

**Labor:** Labor allocated administratively; enterprise employment norms

**Concentrated power:** Central command concentration

**Accountability:** Planning authority and party hierarchy

**Historical exemplars:** Soviet-type economies; Classic central planning textbooks and critiques

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC market freedom and private property foundations

**Strengths:** Mobilization for defined crash goals; Rapid structural shift capacity. **Risks:** Information failures; Shortages; Coercion; Innovation drag.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/command-economies.md`

### [Market socialism](/compare/market-socialism/)
**Ownership:** Social or public ownership of firms that compete in markets

**Markets:** Markets allocate goods; capital ownership socialized

**Government:** Sets ownership rules, regulation, and sometimes investment planning

**Property regime:** Limited for means of production; personal property retained

**Labor:** Worker self-management variants or publicly hired labor

**Concentrated power:** Social ownership plus market competition among enterprises

**Accountability:** Boards, planners, or worker councils depending on model

**Historical exemplars:** Lange model debates; Yugoslav self-management literature; Late socialist reform programs

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Still socializes productive capital; CC retains private productive property

**Strengths:** Attempts to combine social ownership with price signals. **Risks:** Soft budgets; Political interference; Investment inefficiency debates.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/market-socialism.md`

### [Syndicalism](/compare/syndicalism/)
**Ownership:** Producer unions / syndicates control industry

**Markets:** Often subordinated to federated producer coordination

**Government:** Skeptical of parliamentary state; dual power via unions

**Property regime:** Rejected for productive assets

**Labor:** Workers as collective industrial sovereigns

**Concentrated power:** Union federation replaces capital owners

**Accountability:** Union democracy and industrial federations

**Historical exemplars:** French CGT revolutionary syndicalism; Spanish CNT traditions; IWW influence

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Rejects private capital sovereignty; incompatible with CC property foundations

**Strengths:** Workplace democracy ideal; Direct producer power. **Risks:** Producer capture against consumers; Coordination across industries; Militancy-institutionalization tension.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/syndicalism.md`

### [Mutualism](/compare/mutualism/)
**Ownership:** Possessory use-rights and mutual credit; anti-capitalist rent/interest critique

**Markets:** Markets of independent producers without capitalist profit on capital

**Government:** Minimal or federative; anti-authoritarian

**Property regime:** Possession of use; hostility to absentee capitalist property

**Labor:** Independent producers and mutual associations

**Concentrated power:** Mutual credit and anti-monopoly norms

**Accountability:** Contracts among free producers and mutual banks

**Historical exemplars:** P.J. Proudhon's writings; Mutual credit experiments

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Shares anti-domination instincts but rejects CC's acceptance of productive private capital within constitutional limits

**Strengths:** Anti-authoritarian socialism; Producer independence ethic. **Risks:** Credit system fragility; Scale limits; Ambiguity vs markets.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/mutualism.md`

### [State capitalism](/compare/state-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** State owns or controls key enterprises while using market or quasi-market tools

**Markets:** Markets may operate under state direction

**Government:** State is owner, regulator, and strategist

**Property regime:** Limited or subordinate in commanding heights

**Labor:** Labor often state-directed or weakly independent

**Concentrated power:** State concentration of economic power

**Accountability:** State authority and performance legitimacy

**Historical exemplars:** Debates over USSR as state capitalism; Contemporary SOE-heavy marketizers

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC independent property and constitutional limits on state economic domination

**Strengths:** Mobilization; Strategic investment capacity. **Risks:** Political coercion; Weak independent accountability; Soft budgets.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/state-capitalism.md`

## Diagnostic

### [Crony capitalism](/compare/crony-capitalism/)
**Ownership:** Nominally private ownership with privileged political access

**Markets:** Markets distorted by favors, barriers, and selective enforcement

**Government:** Distributes privileges, bailouts, and protection to connected firms

**Property regime:** Formal but selectively applied

**Labor:** Labor subject to privileged firms' market power

**Concentrated power:** Protects favored incumbents

**Accountability:** Patronage networks

**Historical exemplars:** Privilege-ridden developmental episodes; Regulatory capture case literatures

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Directly contrary to CC

**Strengths:** Can mobilize aligned investment quickly; Predictability for insiders. **Risks:** Corruption; Weak competition; Public cynicism.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/crony-capitalism.md`

### [Oligarchy](/compare/oligarchy/)
**Ownership:** Ownership and control concentrated among a small elite

**Markets:** Markets subordinate to elite coordination when they conflict with elite interests

**Government:** Government captured or directed by the few

**Property regime:** Selective—strong for elites

**Labor:** Labor dependent on elite institutions

**Concentrated power:** Concentration entrenched as governing principle

**Accountability:** Elite cohesion and coercion

**Historical exemplars:** Classical Greek oligarchic constitutions (concept); Modern 'oligarch' literatures in post-Soviet and other contexts

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC anti-concentration and equal citizenship

**Strengths:** Fast elite coordination; Policy stability for insiders. **Risks:** Domination; Weak accountability; Exit of talent and capital over time.

### [Plutocracy](/compare/plutocracy/)
**Ownership:** Wealth holders dominate ownership and politics

**Markets:** Markets reinforce wealth-to-power translation

**Government:** Disproportionately responsive to wealth

**Property regime:** Strong for elites

**Labor:** Labor voice diminished relative to capital

**Concentrated power:** Money translates into political control

**Accountability:** Campaign finance, lobbying, and elite media influence (mechanisms vary)

**Historical exemplars:** Progressive Era critiques; Contemporary money-in-politics research programs

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC separation of economic power from civic domination

**Strengths:** Investment capacity; Policy attention to capital formation. **Risks:** Political inequality; Capture; Erosion of equal citizenship.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/plutocracy.md`

### [Kleptocracy](/compare/kleptocracy/)
**Ownership:** Public office used to seize and launder private fortunes

**Markets:** Markets become channels for looting and offshore concealment

**Government:** Government is the extraction machine

**Property regime:** Insecure for subjects; hyper-secure for protected looters

**Labor:** Labor and citizens as fiscal prey

**Concentrated power:** Theft centralized in ruling networks

**Accountability:** Fear, patronage, and opacity

**Historical exemplars:** Comparative kleptocracy case studies; Illicit financial flows literatures

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Maximally contrary to constitutional accountable government and lawful property

**Strengths:** Enrichment of rulers; Short-term loyalty buying. **Risks:** State failure risk; Capital flight; Violence; Development collapse.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/kleptocracy.md`

## Political-economic

### [Fascist political economy / fascist corporatism](/compare/fascist-political-economy/)
**Ownership:** Private property retained under state-dictated corporatist coordination

**Markets:** Markets subordinated to autarky, militarization, and party goals

**Government:** Dictatorial party-state directs syndicates and firms

**Property regime:** Formally private; politically conditional

**Labor:** Independent unions crushed; compulsory syndicates

**Concentrated power:** State-party concentration; cartels under political command

**Accountability:** Leader and party; terror against opponents

**Historical exemplars:** Italian Fascist corporatist institutions; Nazi economic controls (related but not identical)

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Absolutely contrary to CC's constitutional liberty and anti-domination

**Strengths:** Rapid militarized mobilization (historical); Suppression of left labor conflict (from regime view). **Risks:** Totalitarian violence; War; Destruction of pluralism; Economic irrationality under politics.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/fascist-political-economy.md`

### [Authoritarian market economy / illiberal capitalism](/compare/authoritarian-market-economy/)
**Ownership:** Private and state ownership under authoritarian political monopoly

**Markets:** Product and labor markets operate; politics closed

**Government:** Authoritarian center sets red lines; selectively intervenes in firms

**Property regime:** Real but politically contingent

**Labor:** Markets with restricted independent organizing

**Concentrated power:** Political concentration primary; economic concentration tolerated or managed

**Accountability:** Performance and coercion more than open contestation

**Historical exemplars:** Comparative 'authoritarian capitalism' case literatures; Illiberal market reformers

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Rejects CC's democratic constitutional accountability even if markets exist

**Strengths:** Policy continuity; Rapid projects without electoral vetoes (regime claim). **Risks:** Rights abuses; Succession crises; Crony/kleptocratic drift; Information distortion.

Full dossier: `content/public-resources/systems/authoritarian-market-economy.md`
