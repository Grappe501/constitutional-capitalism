# Economic System Comparison

> **Developing analytical tool — not settled scholarship.** This comparison is a Phase 1 working aid, not a definitive account of any tradition. It uses concise descriptions, omits internal diversity, and will be revised after sourcing and critique.

Constitutional Capitalism is a developing framework, not a settled ideology. It protects private property and enterprise while asking how constitutional law can prevent both public and private domination.

## Laissez-faire capitalism
**Ownership:** Predominantly private ownership

**Markets:** Markets coordinate most activity with minimal intervention

**Government:** Limited to property, contract, and basic order

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** A partial ancestor; CC adds constitutional accountability

**Strengths:** Entrepreneurship; low intervention. **Risks:** Externalities; concentrated power.

## Shareholder-primacy capitalism
**Ownership:** Private corporate ownership oriented to shareholders

**Markets:** Markets allocate capital and corporate control

**Government:** Sets corporate and securities law

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects shareholder value as the sole constitutional lens

**Strengths:** Clear managerial objective; capital formation. **Risks:** Short-termism; stakeholder externalities.

## Crony capitalism
**Ownership:** Nominally private ownership with privileged access

**Markets:** Markets distorted by political favors

**Government:** Distributes privileges and protection

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Directly contrary to CC

**Strengths:** Can mobilize investment quickly. **Risks:** Corruption; weak competition.

## Corporatism
**Ownership:** Private or mixed ownership organized by functional groups

**Markets:** Markets coordinated through organized interests

**Government:** Bargains with and directs recognized groups

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC may learn from coordination but rejects closed privilege

**Strengths:** Coordination; representation. **Risks:** Exclusion; elite bargaining.

## Oligarchy
**Ownership:** Ownership concentrated among a small elite

**Markets:** Markets subordinate to elite control

**Government:** Government captured or directed by elite

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC anti-concentration

**Strengths:** Fast elite coordination. **Risks:** Domination; weak accountability.

## Plutocracy
**Ownership:** Wealth holders dominate ownership and politics

**Markets:** Markets reinforce wealth influence

**Government:** Government responsive disproportionately to wealth

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC separation principle

**Strengths:** Investment capacity. **Risks:** Political inequality; capture.

## Mercantilism
**Ownership:** Mixed private ownership under national strategic direction

**Markets:** Trade managed for national advantage

**Government:** Uses tariffs, monopolies, and charters

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects privilege while allowing lawful resilience

**Strengths:** State capacity; industrial development. **Risks:** Rent seeking; conflict.

## State capitalism
**Ownership:** State owns or controls key enterprises

**Markets:** Markets may operate under state direction

**Government:** State is owner, regulator, and strategist

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC independent property and limits

**Strengths:** Mobilization; strategic investment. **Risks:** Political coercion; weak independent accountability.

## Social democracy
**Ownership:** Mostly private ownership with strong public welfare

**Markets:** Markets remain central

**Government:** Provides services, redistribution, and labor protections

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC overlaps on accountability but remains distinct

**Strengths:** Security; equality of opportunity. **Risks:** Fiscal burden; administrative complexity.

## Democratic socialism
**Ownership:** Substantial social or worker ownership

**Markets:** Markets vary by program

**Government:** Democratic direction of economy

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC protects private property and is not this system

**Strengths:** Equality; workplace voice. **Risks:** State or collective control risks.

## Socialism
**Ownership:** Social, cooperative, or state ownership of major productive assets

**Markets:** Markets range from limited to absent

**Government:** Directs or owns significant production

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC is not socialism

**Strengths:** Equality; coordination. **Risks:** Innovation and liberty risks.

## Marxism
**Ownership:** Collective ownership after class transformation

**Markets:** Markets seen as historically superseded

**Government:** State often transitional in theory

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects class-revolutionary premises

**Strengths:** Structural critique of exploitation. **Risks:** Authoritarian implementation risks.

## Communism
**Ownership:** Common ownership in a classless ideal

**Markets:** Markets abolished in ideal form

**Government:** State withers away in theory

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC rejects abolition of property and markets

**Strengths:** Egalitarian ideal. **Risks:** Coordination and coercion risks in practice.

## Command economies
**Ownership:** State ownership or command over production

**Markets:** Planning replaces market allocation

**Government:** Central planners direct output and prices

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Contrary to CC market freedom

**Strengths:** Mobilization for defined goals. **Risks:** Information failures; coercion.

## Welfare capitalism
**Ownership:** Private ownership with employer or public benefits

**Markets:** Markets central

**Government:** Supports income and social insurance

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC may incorporate compatible protections

**Strengths:** Security within markets. **Risks:** Dependency on employers; cost pressures.

## Stakeholder capitalism
**Ownership:** Private ownership with multiple stakeholder duties

**Markets:** Markets central

**Government:** Sets disclosure and fiduciary expectations

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC seeks constitutional grounding for accountability

**Strengths:** Broader impact recognition. **Risks:** Vagueness; weak enforcement.

## Distributism
**Ownership:** Widely distributed small-scale property

**Markets:** Markets with preference for small owners

**Government:** Supports ownership dispersion and associations

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC shares broad ownership but not a fixed form

**Strengths:** Independence; local ownership. **Risks:** Scale limitations; romanticism.

## Cooperative economics
**Ownership:** Member-owned firms and associations

**Markets:** Markets can coordinate cooperatives

**Government:** Enables cooperative legal structures

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** CC treats cooperatives as one possible mechanism

**Strengths:** Participation; shared returns. **Risks:** Capital access; governance challenges.

## Social-market economics
**Ownership:** Private ownership within a social legal order

**Markets:** Markets coordinate under social safeguards

**Government:** Maintains competition and social protection

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** A close comparative reference, not an identity

**Strengths:** Competitive dynamism; social security. **Risks:** Complexity; fiscal demands.

## Ordoliberalism
**Ownership:** Private ownership under a competitive order

**Markets:** Markets need an ordering legal framework

**Government:** Strong rule-setting state, limited discretionary direction

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** A major intellectual comparison for CC

**Strengths:** Competition; anti-monopoly clarity. **Risks:** May understate distributional issues.

## Public-benefit capitalism
**Ownership:** Private ownership including mission-locked firms

**Markets:** Markets with social-purpose corporate forms

**Government:** Authorizes and regulates benefit entities

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** Compatible mechanism, not complete CC system

**Strengths:** Mission protection; innovation. **Risks:** Voluntary uptake; enforcement.

## Constitutional Capitalism
**Ownership:** Secure private and broadly distributed ownership

**Markets:** Free and fair markets under constitutional safeguards

**Government:** Limited, capable, democratically accountable rule enforcement

**Relationship to Constitutional Capitalism:** The developing framework being defined

**Strengths:** Liberty; pluralism; broad opportunity. **Risks:** Unresolved mechanisms; overbreadth risk.
