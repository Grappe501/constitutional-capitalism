# Argument Layers Standard

Last updated: 2026-08-04

Constitutional Capitalism must distinguish four layers of assertion in every public text, manuscript unit, and dashboard claim.

## 1. Principles

What ought to guide a free republic’s economic institutions.

- Normative and constitutional in character
- Derived from the Declaration and principles register
- May be debated, but are not presented as empirical forecasts

## 2. Proposals

What the project recommends for consideration, piloting, or eventual legislation.

- Contingent on design details
- Downstream of principles
- Must not be treated as already enacted or proven

## 3. Predictions

What the project expects *might* happen if the framework is implemented well.

- Explicitly labeled as predictions or scenarios
- Require future modeling, pilots, and measurement
- Must not be presented as settled evidence

## 4. Evidence

What can presently be demonstrated with sources.

- Lives in the claim ledger with source IDs
- Subject to fact-check and legal-review flags
- Absence of evidence must remain visible

## Framing rule

Prefer:

> This is a proposed constitutional framework intended to strengthen the conditions for a freer, more prosperous, and more resilient society.

Avoid overclaiming:

> This will create a greater nation.

## Organizing identity (working)

A candidate organizing principle for the book:

> Shift the question from redistributing wealth after it is created to broadening participation in creating and owning wealth in the first place — from a wage-based middle class toward an ownership-based middle class.

This identity is recorded as a working thesis (`CC-DEC-026`). It does not replace the canonical definition or central belief.
