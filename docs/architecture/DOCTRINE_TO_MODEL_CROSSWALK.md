# Doctrine → Model Crosswalk

**Status:** canonical planning artifact; pre-code  
**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Purpose:** Translate Constitutional Capitalism doctrine, hypotheses, and public-site domains into measurable variables, causal questions, data requirements, and eventual Systems Intelligence simulations.

## Governing rule

The model may test Constitutional Capitalism. It may not assume Constitutional Capitalism works.

Every causal path must distinguish fact, institutional estimate, established causal relationship, supported inference, observed correlation, plausible mechanism, project assumption, illustrative parameter, unresolved hypothesis, and normative proposal. Unknown parameters remain unknown until sourced or explicitly entered as scenario assumptions.

The National Debt & Prosperity Model is the fiscal/economic spine of the existing **Systems Intelligence Engine**, not a competing architecture. The existing engine remains the whole-system capstone; this work prepares its model contracts and evidence requirements before code.

## Universal causal chain

Policy / investment → institutional output → household/community change → population/labor/capital response → productivity/market response → tax base/public cost response → federal/state/local fiscal response → debt/interest/public asset response → human flourishing → intergenerational effect.

Every intervention must also expose who pays, who benefits, timing, geographic distribution, dependencies, failure modes, legal constraints, uncertainty, and cost shifting between households and levels of government.

## Required cross-system simulations

### Rural repopulation and distributed prosperity

Model regional academies + transportation + broadband + housing + healthcare + childcare + employers + ownership + food systems as a system, not independent sliders. Track Keep / Return / Attract separately and by age, household type, income, profession, and family composition.

### Children as infrastructure

Model maternal health, nutrition, early learning, school quality, mental health, housing, civic education, and apprenticeships as long-duration capability investment. Do not reduce childhood worth to economic productivity; fiscal and economic effects are only one layer of the flourishing analysis.

### Productive public debt

Separate consumption, maintenance, capability investment, physical capital, institutional capital, risk reduction, and revenue-producing assets. Track the liability and the asset/output it finances. Compare upfront borrowing and interest with public asset value, private capital response, productivity, operating savings, tax revenue, resilience, and long-run net public position.

### Prevention dividend

For health, childhood development, fire/EMS, disaster preparedness, food resilience, and maintenance, explicitly model `cost_now → avoided_loss_later`, with uncertainty bands and counterfactual baseline.

### Local wealth retention

Track where profits, wages, equity, procurement dollars, processing margins, royalties, and investment returns ultimately reside. National GDP alone cannot answer whether a community is becoming wealthier.

### AI transition and abundance

Model productivity gains and labor displacement simultaneously. Include reskilling capacity, labor-share changes, ownership concentration/distribution, energy load, tax effects, assistance transition costs, new enterprise formation, and geographic distribution.

## Rose Bud agricultural academy scenario contract

The academy is a model case, not a predicted success.

**Intervention:** regional agricultural / food / logistics / technology magnet academy.

**Initial costs:** site, construction, equipment, staff, transport, student supports, utilities, housing if applicable, and operating subsidy.

**Direct outputs:** student seats, school jobs, apprenticeships, student enterprises, employer/farm partnerships, credentials, research/demo capacity.

**First-order effects:** skill formation, local purchasing, employment, school enrollment, commuting patterns, housing demand.

**Second-order effects:** graduate retention, return migration, farm succession, entrepreneurship, processing capacity, employer formation, healthcare/retail viability.

**Third-order effects:** young-family population, family proximity, community age structure, property values, local tax base, institutional capacity, regional specialization, local ownership.

**Long-run outputs:** 30-, 50-, and 70-year scenario bands; cumulative public cost/revenue; public and private assets; household wealth; population composition; intergenerational continuity; human-flourishing profile.

**Complements that must be modeled:** housing, healthcare, childcare, broadband, transportation, employers, land access, business capital, processing markets, water/energy capacity.

**Failure modes:** trains students to leave; housing blocks population growth; weak employer demand; high recurring cost; duplicated regional capacity; transport barrier; financing barrier; land-access barrier; local displacement/gentrification; inadequate staffing.

No migration elasticity, multiplier, retention percentage, lifetime fiscal value, or 70-year effect is allowed to become a default parameter until supported by evidence or explicitly labeled as a user-entered scenario assumption.

## Public simulator display contract

Every major result should be viewable as:

**Current → Transition → Mature State**

and at minimum across 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, and 70 years where the underlying model supports that horizon. Long horizons must widen uncertainty rather than imply false precision.

Every output should support geographic views from national to state, region, county, and community where data quality permits.

The public interface should always separate:

- **Fact** — observed/sourced.
- **Estimate** — institutional projection or empirical estimate.
- **Model** — calculation from explicit assumptions.
- **Proposal** — Constitutional Capitalism recommendation.
