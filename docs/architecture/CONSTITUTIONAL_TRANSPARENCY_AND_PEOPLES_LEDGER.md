## Position in the philosophy

This is the **fourth institutional spine** of Constitutional Capitalism — beside justice, democracy, and public service.

Not “everything government does is public.”

A **constitutional transparency principle**: openness by presumption, with limited, reviewable exceptions for rights, privacy, safety, and national security.

## Guiding principle

> In a constitutional republic, government does not own public information. It holds public information in trust for the people, subject only to those limited protections necessary to preserve constitutional rights, legitimate privacy, public safety, and national security.

## Openness presumption

> Government information created with public resources should be presumed open unless there is a constitutionally or legally justified reason to protect it. The burden should generally be on the government to explain why information is withheld.

Legitimate exception categories include classified national security information, active criminal investigations, certain personal records, protected health information, trade secrets submitted under law, and disclosures that would create serious safety or privacy harms.

## The People's Ledger

Named constitutional accountability ledger — not finance alone.

Every public dollar and major public action should have a searchable story: authority, purpose, cost, contracts, payments, audits, and outcomes over time.

Status: **design agenda** — architecture before an operating statewide portal. No invented coverage statistics.

## Digital trail

Every expenditure, vote, contract, grant, regulation, meeting, study, audit, permit, major decision, and publicly funded project should leave a reviewable digital trail.

## Equal access and identity balance

Transparency must not depend on owning a computer. Free public-access terminals in libraries, courthouses, city halls, and state offices.

Routine public records: no unnecessary identity barriers.  
Sensitive or high-volume access: authentication/rate limits where needed.  
Actions that change records: always authenticated and audited.

## AI

AI may help citizens query and understand public records. The records remain the official source. AI does not replace government.

## Constitutional Transparency Test

Ten questions (`CC-TTEST-01`–`10`) screen reforms for purpose, measurability, spending reviewability, error challenge, lawful withholding, audit trails, and intergenerational understanding.

Research precedes detailed open-records, procurement-publication, or portal legislation.
