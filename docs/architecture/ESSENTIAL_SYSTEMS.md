## Position in the philosophy

Banking, insurance, healthcare, and pharmaceuticals belong in Constitutional Capitalism — framed as **system design**, not as predetermined conclusions of inherent corruption.

Ask:

> What incentives produce outcomes that do not align well with the public interest, and how can we redesign those incentives?

## Constitutional question

> How should a society design essential systems so that private innovation and investment are rewarded while the public interest remains protected?

The same question evaluates banking, insurance, and healthcare — they are not special cases outside the framework.

## Chapter method

Each essential-system chapter answers four questions before proposing reforms:

1. What is this system supposed to accomplish?  
2. How does it currently function?  
3. Where are the incentives well aligned, and where are they not?  
4. What reforms would better align the system with the principles of Constitutional Capitalism?

## Core principles

**Banking / finance (`CC-DEV-034`):** The financial system should primarily reward the creation of productive value rather than the extraction of value.

**Healthcare (`CC-DEV-035`):** Healthy citizens are not simply beneficiaries of society — they are one of its greatest strengths. Measure success not only by treating illness, but by helping people remain healthy.

**Preventive society (`CC-DEV-036`):** Shift public investment gradually from paying for avoidable crises toward preventing those crises — across healthcare, education, justice, infrastructure, disaster preparedness, environment, and economic development.

## Named instrument

**Community Health Index** — design agenda for community competition around health outcomes (life expectancy, prevention, access, environment, emergency response), not merely hospital size. No invented scores.

## Pharmaceuticals

Acknowledge life-extending innovation. Prefer researchable questions about incentives, prevention vs treatment weighting, pricing transparency, publicly funded research, and affordability — not unsupported claims that the system is designed to keep people sick.

## Essential Systems Test

Ten questions (`CC-ETEST-01`–`10`) screen industries for long-term value, competition, informed choice, incentive alignment, community strength, transparency, risk/reward fairness, place investment, and measurable life improvement.

Research precedes detailed banking, insurance, healthcare, or pharmaceutical legislation.
