## Position in the philosophy

This is the **public-service and institutional-modernization doctrine** of Constitutional Capitalism — a third institutional spine beside justice and democracy.

Not shrink-until-broken. Not bureaucracy-for-its-own-sake.

Build government that is technologically modern, easier to understand and audit, staffed by capable citizens, visibly connected to communities, and focused on service rather than institutional self-preservation.

## Foundational principle

> Government work should be among the highest forms of civic service. Public institutions should use technology to eliminate unnecessary administrative labor so human talent can be concentrated in judgment, problem-solving, public engagement, oversight, and service.

## Operational rule

> Use technology to remove unnecessary bureaucracy, use human talent where judgment and compassion matter, and keep every public institution visible to the people who own it.

## Technology doctrine

> Automate the routine. Elevate the human.

Guardrail: every digital service includes a real human pathway for elderly, disabled, rural, poor, offline, or digitally uncomfortable citizens.

## Arkansas objective (model jurisdiction)

> Build a state government that is small enough to understand, capable enough to serve, transparent enough to trust, and open enough for ordinary citizens to enter and improve.

Design agendas include a **Public Authority Inventory** and an **Arkansas Civic Service Credential**. Neither is executed here; do not invent board counts or program results.

## Research before policy

Architecture and developing doctrine only. Detailed civil-service codes, statewide IT procurement mandates, and board-abolition packages wait for Phase 2.1 sourced diagnosis.
