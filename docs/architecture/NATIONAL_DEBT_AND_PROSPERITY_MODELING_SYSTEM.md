# National Debt & Prosperity Modeling System

Status: PRE-CODE ARCHITECTURE
Date: 2026-08-28

## Purpose

Create a public-facing, evidence-grounded systems model that explains the United States economy, federal budget, banking system, Treasury market, national debt, public investment, and Constitutional Capitalism proposals in plain English while allowing users to explore long-run consequences through transparent simulation.

This is not a debt calculator. It is intended to become a causal public model of the national economy and of the transition toward Constitutional Capitalism.

The modeling system must connect policy inputs to household, community, regional, state, and national consequences over 1-, 5-, 10-, 30-, 50-, and 70-year horizons where evidence permits.

## Core public comparison

Every major modeled measure should support three states:

1. **Current State** — authoritative observed or baseline data.
2. **Constitutional Capitalism Target** — the project’s proposed destination, when a target has been formally adopted.
3. **User Scenario** — assumptions and policy choices changed interactively by the public.

A fourth layer is always available:

4. **Evidence / Learn More** — plain-English explanation through expert-level sourcing and methodology.

## Non-negotiable epistemic rule

The simulator must never make doctrine look like fact or a hypothesis look like a forecast.

Every displayed relationship, value, target, or outcome must be typed as one of:

- FACT — directly sourced observation.
- ESTIMATE — published institutional estimate or projection.
- MODEL — calculated output from disclosed assumptions.
- HYPOTHESIS — proposed causal relationship requiring research/testing.
- PROPOSAL — Constitutional Capitalism policy or institutional recommendation.
- TARGET — formally selected destination or performance objective.
- UNKNOWN — required variable or relationship not yet supported well enough to model.

Unknowns remain unknown until evidence is added. No fake precision.

## Core causal model

Each Constitutional Capitalism intervention should eventually be represented through a causal chain of the form:

**Policy / investment input**
→ **direct institutional output**
→ **household/community change**
→ **population / labor / capital response**
→ **productivity and market response**
→ **tax-base and public-cost response**
→ **federal/state/local fiscal response**
→ **debt / interest / asset response**
→ **human flourishing response**
→ **intergenerational response**

Not every intervention will have measurable links at every step. Missing links must remain explicit.

## Geographic prosperity and rural repopulation

The model must support hypotheses about changing where Americans can productively live.

Examples include:

- transportation systems connecting urban employment/capital to rural and small-town living
- broadband and digital infrastructure
- attainable housing and multigenerational housing
- regional healthcare access
- childcare
- schools and regional academies
- local food and retail capacity
- small-business formation and ownership
- remote and distributed work
- community institutions and civic networks
- regional economic specialization

The model must be able to ask:

- What causes households to KEEP, RETURN, or ATTRACT into a community?
- Which household types move or remain?
- What happens to local housing demand and supply?
- What happens to wages, labor supply, school enrollment, healthcare demand, local tax base, business formation, property values, transportation demand, and public-service costs?
- Does investment merely relocate activity, or increase net productive capacity?
- What costs are shifted between federal, state, and local government?
- What benefits accrue outside the originating jurisdiction?

## Public investment accounting

The model must distinguish:

1. **Consumption expenditure** — primarily produces current-period services or transfers.
2. **Maintenance expenditure** — preserves existing public or social capital.
3. **Capability investment** — develops human capacity.
4. **Physical capital investment** — creates durable infrastructure or assets.
5. **Institutional investment** — creates long-lived organizational capacity.
6. **Risk-reduction investment** — reduces future expected public/private loss.
7. **Revenue-producing public investment** — may generate fees, royalties, equity, licensing, lease, tax-base, or other direct/indirect returns.

Debt-financed investment must show both the liability and any associated asset/capability pathway.

## Modeling guardrails

- No invented elasticities.
- No invented multipliers.
- No assumed causal effects because a proposal is favored.
- Correlation must not silently become causation.
- Long horizons require wider uncertainty.
- Distributional effects must not disappear into averages.
- Federal savings that create state/local costs must show the transfer.
- State/local savings that require federal spending must show the transfer.
- Public investment returns must separate direct cash return from indirect fiscal return and broader social return.
- A national GDP gain is not sufficient evidence of human flourishing.
- A budget saving is not sufficient evidence of social improvement.
- A human-flourishing improvement is not automatically a fiscal saving.
- Model outputs must expose assumptions and permit alternate assumptions.

## Educational drill-down standard

Every major term or concept should support progressive disclosure:

1. Plain English
2. Learn It
3. Advanced

The deepest level should link definitions, methodology, primary data, research literature, competing interpretations, uncertainty, and the exact way the concept is represented in the model.
