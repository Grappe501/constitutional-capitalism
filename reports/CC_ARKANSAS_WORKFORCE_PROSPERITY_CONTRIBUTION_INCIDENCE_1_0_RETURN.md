## Verdict

Human-capital is a **real scale envelope**, not a tiny base — and still **$0 COUNTABLE NOW**. Private Arkansas wages (~**$64.5B**) and labor compensation (~**$86B**) show large employers could, in principle, finance workforce capacity. But brutal incidence, credit leakage, small-business protection, and legal design are unresolved. Existing corporate income tax (~**$524M**) is **already spent** and is not free WPC capacity.

Design logic locked:

> **Contribute to the shared workforce system OR directly build verified workforce capacity** — not merely raise the corporate tax.

## Credit model

Eligible verified investments: apprenticeships, CTE, university partnerships, rural training, paid internships, tuition support, workforce infrastructure.

**Gross liability ≠ net Tax Retirement Fund cash.** A philosophically correct high-credit design can still produce little IIT-replacement revenue. Credit leakage remains **UNMODELED**.

## Incidence schema (required on every design)

`statutory payer → likely economic payer → worker effect → consumer-price effect → investment effect → small-business effect → competitiveness effect → revenue stability`

Small/family businesses stay **structurally protected by default** unless evidence says otherwise. Target: corporate scale that materially depends on Arkansas’s publicly built human capital.
