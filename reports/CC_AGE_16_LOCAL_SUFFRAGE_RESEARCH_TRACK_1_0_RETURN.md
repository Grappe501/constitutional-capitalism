## Scope executed

Upgrade age-16 local suffrage from a speculative card to a **serious comparative empirical research track** — **not doctrine**, **not a principle**, **not locked voting-age policy**. No election ingest. No new Evidence Panel. Structural next (FRED/BEA) unchanged.

## Central question

> Under what institutional conditions does enfranchisement at 16 produce meaningful, durable civic participation?

## Hard constraint locked

> Educate everyone; enfranchise by law — not by examination.

## Four propositions (separate verdict slots)

1. **Capability** — quality of vote choice  
2. **Participation** — registration/turnout vs overall and 18–24  
3. **Habit formation** — persistence after 18  
4. **System effects** — schools, campaigns, journalism, families, candidates, local government  

## Sequencing assumption demoted to a test

Prior preference (“civic education first, then suffrage”) is now an explicit research question. Reality decides whether enfranchisement itself makes civic education more effective.
