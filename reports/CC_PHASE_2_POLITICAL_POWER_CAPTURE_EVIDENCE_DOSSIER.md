## Research Q&A

### What can empirical literature actually establish about money and political influence?

Disclosure systems (FEC, LDA) establish descriptive facts about money and lobbying activity. Peer-reviewed work (Gilens/Page) supports unequal policy responsiveness under specific designs. Congruence reanalyses (Branham/Soroka/Wlezien) limit strong oligarchy readings. None of these alone establish national 'capture' from economic concentration.

### What evidence demonstrates association?

Gilens & Page 2014: elite/business-group preferences associated with policy outcomes independent of average citizens. FEC/LDA: observable money and lobbying volumes.

### What evidence supports causal inference?

Limited. Preferential correlation and identification debates remain. Campaign-finance shocks and lobbying studies exist in literature but are not yet registered as a full causal spine here. Capture causal claims at national level remain under-identified for CC-CLAIM-003.

### What constitutes 'capture' in political science/economics literature?

Regulatory capture: industry control of its regulator. State capture: systemic private shaping of rules. Neither equals campaign contributions or unequal responsiveness.

### Is capture measurable at the national level?

Debated. National indices and case studies exist; strong national capture claims require operational definition + identification. Current CC sources do not meet that bar.

### What evidence exists at state/local levels?

Not yet registered as Phase 2 primary modules; FEC is federal; LDA is federal. State capture/local lobbying remain open gaps.

### What evidence contradicts strong capture claims?

Branham, Soroka & Wlezien 2017: when groups disagree, rich do not dominate; preference alignment is common.

### What alternative explanations exist?

Status-quo bias; preference coincidence across income groups; party/ideology; organized labor/mass groups; institutional veto points.

### What wording would be defensible if CC-CLAIM-003 remains too broad?

Split into: (1) wealth/economic resource concentration (sourced); (2) unequal policy responsiveness (Gilens/Page + critiques); (3) lobbying/contribution descriptive facts (FEC/LDA); (4) regulatory/state capture as separate claims requiring agency/sector evidence. Do not use 'capture' as umbrella.
